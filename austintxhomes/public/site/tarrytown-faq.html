Tarrytown Basics

Where the neighborhood is, what it costs, and how it's structured.

Where is Tarrytown in Austin?

Tarrytown is a central-west Austin neighborhood, roughly bounded by MoPac (Loop 1) to the west, Lake Austin to the south, and Enfield Road to the north. Zip code is 78703. It sits just west of downtown Austin and just north of Lady Bird Lake.

What is the median home price in Tarrytown Austin?

Median Tarrytown home prices run roughly $1.1M to $1.5M. Entry-tier homes start around $700K to $800K; premium properties reach $3M to $5M+ for Upper Tarrytown estates and lakefront positions. Price varies dramatically by block, lot characteristics, and renovation quality. See the Tarrytown Market Report for current MLS data.

What zip code is Tarrytown Austin?

Tarrytown is in zip code 78703. However, 78703 includes several other neighborhoods — Clarksville, Bryker Woods, Old Enfield. Not everything in 78703 is Tarrytown.

Is Tarrytown walkable?

Moderately. Walk Score is roughly 72. You can walk to Deep Eddy Pool, Pease Park, several coffee shops and restaurants, and Casis Elementary from many blocks. It's not a walking-everywhere neighborhood like Travis Heights, but daily errands and leisure are often car-optional.

How close is Tarrytown to downtown Austin?

Roughly 5 to 10 minutes by car. Southern Tarrytown near Windsor Road is approximately 1.5 miles from the heart of downtown Austin. Access via Lake Austin Boulevard, Enfield Road, and MoPac.

Is Tarrytown safe?

Consistently one of the safest neighborhoods in central Austin. Residential, quiet, and closely watched by neighbors. Standard urban precautions apply, but overall crime rates are meaningfully below Austin averages.

What amenities are near Tarrytown?

Deep Eddy Pool (historic spring-fed public pool, walking distance from many Tarrytown blocks), Lady Bird Lake and the Hike-and-Bike Trail, Pease Park, Reed Park, Mayfield Park (with peacocks), Lake Austin marinas and access points, Fresh Plus grocery, several neighborhood coffee shops and restaurants, and easy MoPac access to downtown and the Domain.

Tarrytown Schools

The single most consequential question for family buyers. See the full Tarrytown Schools Guide for the deep dive.

What school district serves Tarrytown?

Most of Tarrytown is served by Austin ISD, feeding into Casis Elementary, O. Henry Middle School, and Austin High School. A small pocket of western Tarrytown falls within Eanes ISD, feeding into Bridge Point Elementary, West Ridge Middle, and Westlake High School. Verify each address individually.

Is Casis Elementary the school for all of Tarrytown?

No. Most of Tarrytown feeds into Casis, but some southern and eastern-edge addresses feed into Matthews Elementary. Confirm the exact assignment for any specific Tarrytown address using the AISD boundary map before assuming Casis. Luke Allen pulls this on every property.

Is Casis Elementary really that good?

Yes — by measurable standards. Casis consistently ranks among the top public elementaries in Austin ISD by test scores, parent surveys, and demographic outcomes. It has strong community engagement and long institutional continuity. Other AISD elementaries in the area (Bryker Woods, Highland Park) also perform well.

What middle school and high school do Tarrytown kids attend?

Most Tarrytown families feed O. Henry Middle → Austin High School. Some blocks feed Lamar Middle instead. Casis students can also apply to Kealing Middle's competitive magnet program. Eanes-boundary parcels feed West Ridge Middle → Westlake High.

What private schools are near Tarrytown?

St. Andrew's Episcopal (K-12, on the Tarrytown edge), St. Michael's Catholic Academy (9-12), Regents School of Austin (classical Christian K-12), Trinity Episcopal (K-8), Headwaters (progressive K-8), and St. Austin Catholic (K-8) are all within 15 minutes of Tarrytown. Tarrytown is one of the strongest private-school-adjacent neighborhoods in Austin.

Can I transfer into Casis if I don't live in-zone?

Rarely. Casis is at or near capacity most years, so out-of-zone transfers are seldom granted. The reliable path in is to live in-zone — which is one reason Tarrytown addresses inside the Casis boundary carry a price premium.

Tarrytown Homes & Lots

What the housing stock looks like and what to expect.

Are there new construction homes in Tarrytown?

Yes, but exclusively as teardown-and-rebuild on existing lots. There are no new subdivisions in Tarrytown — the neighborhood is fully built out. Builders buy older homes on valuable lots, demolish them, and construct new custom homes.

What's the deal with Tarrytown foundations?

Most Tarrytown homes sit on Austin's expansive clay soils that swell in wet seasons and contract in dry ones. This causes foundation movement in almost every older Tarrytown home. Some movement is cosmetic and normal; some is serious and expensive. A specialist inspector (not a generalist) is essential.

What's the Heritage Tree Ordinance in Tarrytown?

Austin's Heritage Tree Ordinance protects trees with trunk diameter of 19 inches or greater. Removing a Heritage Tree requires a city permit and often replacement mitigation. For rebuilds or additions, Heritage Tree placement can significantly constrain what is buildable. Luke Allen flags Heritage Tree constraints on every relevant property.

Are there any Tarrytown historic districts?

Some individual Tarrytown properties carry historic-landmark designation, and small pockets have historic-look deed restrictions from original 1920s platting. There is no single continuous Tarrytown historic district. Luke Allen confirms historic designations for specific properties before buyers commit.

Does Tarrytown have flood zones?

Yes. Portions of lower Tarrytown near the creek corridors are FEMA-designated flood zones. Pull the flood map for any specific address; obtain a flood insurance quote before making an offer if applicable.

How does the Tarrytown teardown market work?

Builders buy older homes for the lot value rather than the improvements, demolish the structure, and build new custom homes. Economics depend on lot size, foundation removal complexity, Heritage Tree constraints, current construction costs, and finished-value expectations. Tarrytown vs. Other Neighborhoods

The comparisons Tarrytown buyers actually run.

What's the difference between Tarrytown and Westlake?

Tarrytown is inside Austin city limits, mostly Austin ISD (Casis + Austin High), older-growth character homes on smaller lots, 5-10 min drive from downtown. Westlake is a separate municipality west of MoPac, Eanes ISD (Westlake HS), larger lots with newer construction, more suburban feel. Different neighborhoods for different lifestyles.

What's the difference between Tarrytown and Clarksville?

Tarrytown is larger, has bigger lots, feeds primarily into Casis Elementary, and has a quieter residential feel. Clarksville sits immediately south, is smaller and denser, historically African American, more urban and walkable, feeds into different schools. Prices per square foot are similar; Clarksville lots are typically smaller.

Tarrytown vs. Bryker Woods?

Bryker Woods sits east of Tarrytown, smaller footprint, feeds primarily into Bryker Woods Elementary (also highly rated). More consistent housing stock than Tarrytown's varied mix. Generally slightly more affordable per square foot. Both are excellent central Austin neighborhoods with different feels.

Tarrytown vs. Rosedale?

Rosedale is north of Tarrytown, closer to Central Market and 38th Street. More varied housing stock, generally more affordable, feeds into different AISD schools. Great neighborhood; different vibe from Tarrytown's mature-oak, established-luxury character.

Tarrytown vs. Old Enfield?

Old Enfield sits east of Tarrytown, smaller and denser, closest to downtown, primarily historic 1920s-era homes. Feeds different schools (Bryker Woods Elementary). Distinct character; often compared with Tarrytown because both offer central-Austin historic homes.

Practical Details

Taxes, HOAs, investment outlook.

Does Tarrytown have an HOA?

No. Tarrytown has no HOA. Neighborhood character is maintained through Austin city zoning and deed restrictions that vary by block. Some 1920s-era parcels carry historic-look restrictions; most do not.

What's the Tarrytown property tax rate?

Effective rate runs approximately 1.9% to 2.2% of assessed value annually, combining City of Austin, Travis County, Austin ISD, and other district levies. Homestead exemption reduces taxable value significantly once filed. Texas has no state income tax to offset.

Is Tarrytown a good investment?

Historically strong. Fixed supply, consistent demand, top-rated schools, and central location have driven steady appreciation over decades. Generally a long-hold neighborhood — 7 to 10 year horizons have performed well across market cycles.

When should I file for homestead exemption?

File between January 1 and April 30 in the year after you take title (owner-occupied only, on your primary residence). Reduces your school-district taxable value materially and caps annual assessed-value increases at 10%. Free to file directly with the Travis Central Appraisal District — no need to pay a service.

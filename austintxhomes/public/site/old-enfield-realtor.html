Luke's Take on Old Enfield

The Casis Elementary catchment overlaps heavily with Old Enfield, which is a significant value driver. Casis is one of the highest-rated Austin ISD elementaries, and families who prioritize school access will pay a meaningful premium for a home in the Casis walking radius. That premium is real, but it drops off sharply outside the boundary — even a block outside Casis is worth less to the family-buyer pool.

Generational sales are the norm here. When a family that has owned an Old Enfield home for 40+ years sells, the transaction often includes deferred maintenance that will require significant capital in the first 24 months of ownership. Any inspection here should focus on: foundation (many pier-and-beam), electrical (frequently pre-modern-code), and roof (original slate or tile on some homes is beautiful but expensive to maintain).

What is the median home price in Old Enfield?

Preserved cottages typically run $1.5M to $3M. Enfield Road estates (larger lots, historic mansions) trade $4M to $12M+ when they come up, which is only a handful of transactions per year. Off-Enfield cottages have a more predictable comp set than Enfield Road proper.

Is Old Enfield in the Casis Elementary catchment?

Most of Old Enfield falls in the Casis Elementary catchment, one of Austin ISD's highest-rated elementaries. That drives a real value premium for family buyers. Boundaries are address-specific — always verify with Austin ISD by exact address before assuming Casis assignment.

What's the difference between Old Enfield and Tarrytown?

Both are 78703 west-central Austin. Tarrytown is a slightly broader area, more Craftsman and Colonial Revival stock, and includes the Bryker Woods sub-neighborhood. Old Enfield is more architecturally coherent (1910s-1930s core) with the distinctive Enfield Road estate corridor. Buyer character overlaps significantly.

How often do Enfield Road estates sell?

Fewer than 10 Enfield Road transactions in a typical year. Many properties are generational holdings that trade only when a family transitions ownership. When they do come up, the buyer pool is small, discerning, and often cash — very different transaction dynamics than a standard $1.5M cottage listing.

What should a buyer inspect first in an Old Enfield home?

Foundation (many pier-and-beam), electrical (frequently pre-modern-code and needs updating), and roof (original slate or tile is beautiful but expensive to maintain). Any generational-sale home should be budgeted for $75K-$200K of first-year deferred maintenance depending on how well the previous owners maintained it.

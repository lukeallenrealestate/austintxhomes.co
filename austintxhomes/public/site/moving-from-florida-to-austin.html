The driver that most national content misses

Florida's homeowner insurance crisis is the primary FL-to-Austin driver in 2026

Florida homeowner insurance premiums have risen 100 to 300 percent in many metros since 2022 as major private insurers exited after Hurricane Ian, Idalia, Milton, and Helene. Citizens Property Insurance, the state-run insurer of last resort, now carries well over a million policies. For a Florida family with a $600,000 home paying $8,000 to $12,000 annually in property insurance, the Austin equivalent typically runs $2,000 to $4,000. That is $4,000 to $10,000 of annual carrying-cost savings before anything else enters the picture. It is also the specific reason most of my recent Florida-to-Austin buyers decided to move.

The hurricane-to-hail weather trade, honestly

Most Florida buyers I work with underestimate both sides of the trade when they first start looking at Austin. The honest version:

  • What you give up
    Beach access. Austin has lakes (Lake Austin, Lake Travis) but no ocean. For Florida buyers whose daily life includes beach proximity, this is a real adjustment. The lake lifestyle is different and takes some getting used to.
  • What you give up
    Marine climate. Florida winters are famously mild. Austin winters are mostly mild but have occasional hard freezes and ice events. Summer in both places is hot, but Austin's heat is drier and the sun exposure is intense.
  • What you give up
    Florida food scene. Austin has a strong food scene, but Caribbean, Cuban, and Latin American cuisine traditions available in South Florida and parts of Central Florida are thinner here. BBQ and Tex-Mex are world-class instead.
  • What you get
    No hurricane evacuations. Austin sits far enough inland that direct hurricane impact is rare. No board-ups, no evacuation routes, no insurance claim fights after a storm. For families who have ridden out multiple Florida hurricane seasons, this is a meaningful quality-of-life change.
  • What you get
    Lower insurance carrying cost. $3,000-$8,000 per year of annual savings on a typical home. Over a 10-year ownership, that is $30,000-$80,000 that stays in your pocket instead of flowing to the Florida insurance market.
  • What you get
    Live music, BBQ, and outdoor culture. Austin's music scene is genuinely one of the best in the country. The food scene is strong. Hiking, kayaking on the lakes, and outdoor activity are built into the daily rhythm. Different from Florida's beach-centric lifestyle but comparably good in its own way.
  • What you get
    Property-tax predictability. Higher raw rate than Florida, but the Texas 10 percent homestead cap plus the $100,000 school district homestead exemption produce a stable tax picture once you are established. See the Travis County property tax protest playbook for the full homeowner tax protection stack.

Florida buyers who ride out two more hurricane seasons before deciding usually move. Florida buyers who move once and talk themselves back into staying usually move anyway within three years.

The Florida residency question most buyers get wrong

Because both Florida and Texas have no state income tax, the move does not require formally terminating Florida residency the way a California or New York move does. That is a benefit, and most Florida-to-Austin buyers correctly focus on it. The specific pieces that still matter and that buyers routinely overlook:

  • Florida homestead exemption and Save Our Homes. These are tied to Florida residency and apply only to a primary residence in Florida. You cannot claim homestead on both a Florida property and a Texas property. If you keep a Florida home as a second residence after moving to Austin, you lose the Florida homestead protections on that property.
  • Driver's license and voter registration. Should move to Texas within the first weeks of establishing Texas as your primary residence.
  • Estate planning. Florida and Texas have different estate planning rules, different asset protection rules, and different homestead protections against creditors. If you have meaningful assets or specific estate planning in place, consult a Texas estate attorney shortly after the move.
  • Primary banking and professional relationships. Move the primary banking and any Florida-specific professional relationships (CPA, attorney, financial advisor) to Texas or to national providers with Texas presence.
  • LLC and business entity structures. If you own Florida LLCs or S-corps, discuss with a Texas tax attorney whether to leave them Florida-domiciled or redomicile to Texas.

Timing the Florida sale

Most Florida-to-Austin buyers should sell the Florida property first and buy in Austin with the freed equity, same as the California-to-Austin playbook. Florida-specific timing considerations that CA buyers do not face:

  • Hurricane season (June through November) affects both buyer appetite and listing visibility in many Florida metros. Spring listings (February through May) often perform meaningfully better than fall listings in hurricane-prone areas.
  • Insurance issues on the sale property can slow closings in high-exposure areas. Buyers increasingly ask for insurance quotes before writing offers, and insurability concerns can affect your sale timeline. Document your current coverage and claim history for the buyer package.
  • Recent major storm damage in specific Florida communities has affected the comparable sales analysis in those markets. Price with current comps, not pre-storm comps.

Bottom line

Florida-to-Austin works for most households that make the move in 2026, and it works especially well for families whose current Florida property has an insurance crisis of its own. The insurance delta alone usually makes the financial case before the lifestyle, tax, and employer-market considerations enter the picture. The hurricane-to-hail weather trade is real on both sides, and the honest version is that most families who make the move do not want to go back once they settle in.

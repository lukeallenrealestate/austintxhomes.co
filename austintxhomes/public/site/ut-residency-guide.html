The Basics

How Domicile Determines Your Tuition

Texas law classifies every student as a resident, non-resident, or foreign student. To qualify for in-state rates, you must prove Texas is your permanent home — not merely a place you live while attending school. The most direct path: a parent establishes domicile through Texas residential property ownership.

⚖

Legal Standard

Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board (THECB) rules govern all residency decisions. UT Austin's Office of the Registrar applies these rules to every applicant and is the final decision-maker.

📅

The 12-Month Rule

You must establish and maintain a Texas domicile for 12 consecutive months before the first day of the semester in which you want in-state rates. This clock starts the day you establish qualifying domicile.

🏠

4 Ways to Show Domicile

Texas recognizes four qualifying acts: (1) residential property ownership, (2) Texas business ownership/management, (3) gainful employment, or (4) marriage to a qualifying Texas domiciliary.

Who Qualifies

Dependent vs. Independent Student Paths

Your tax filing status determines which pathway applies to you. Both paths lead to the same outcome — the requirements differ.

Dependent Student

Parent Claims You on Taxes

  • ✓Parent/guardian must physically reside in Texas for 12 consecutive months prior to enrollment
  • ✓Parent/guardian must establish domicile via property ownership, business, or employment
  • ✓Parent must be a U.S. Citizen, Permanent Resident, or eligible visa holder (not F-1, J-1, or TN)
  • ✓Student is claimed on parent's most recent federal tax return
  • ✓Property ownership by the parent — not the student — satisfies domicile for a dependent
Independent Student

You File Your Own Taxes

  • ✓Student must file their own federal tax return and not be claimed as a dependent
  • ✓Student must physically reside in Texas for 12 consecutive months
  • ✓Student must establish domicile via property ownership, business, or employment
  • ✓Student must provide majority of their own financial support
  • ✓Student must be a U.S. Citizen, Permanent Resident, or eligible visa holder
⚠

Financial Aid Warning

Any scholarship or grant that requires out-of-state status will disqualify you from establishing residency. Review your aid package before pursuing this path.

✔

Citizenship Requirement

You must be lawfully present in the U.S. to qualify. F-1, J-1, and TN visa holders are not eligible to establish domicile for tuition purposes.

The Process

Step-by-Step: Property Ownership Path

This is the most common route for out-of-state families. Follow each step precisely — the order and timing matter for your 12-month domicile clock.

1
Before Arrival · Months Before Enrollment

Purchase Residential Real Property in Texas

The property must be residential (a condo, townhome, or single-family home) and must serve as the primary residence of the student (independent) or the parent (dependent). The purchase date on your warranty deed starts your 12-month domicile clock.

Key detail: The property must be your primary residence — not an investment property you don't live in. You must actually establish domicile there.
2
Immediately After Purchase

Establish the Property as Your Primary Residence

Move in and set up all the supporting paper trail from Day 1. This means switching your driver's license, registering to vote in Texas, updating your address with financial institutions, and putting utility accounts in your name.

Pro tip: Start collecting dated documents from the very first month. Gaps in your 12-month paper trail are the #1 reason applications get denied.
3
Ongoing · All 12 Months

Maintain Physical Presence in Texas

Domicile requires ongoing physical presence. Limit extended absences from Texas — UT Austin considers whether you maintained continuous ties to the state. Travel for breaks is generally fine, but you should return to your Texas property.

Important: Actions taken exclusively for educational purposes (living in the dorms, student employment, etc.) do not count toward domicile.
4
Ongoing · All 12 Months

Build Your Documentation File

Every month, collect and organize evidence of your Texas presence. You'll need a continuous 12-month paper trail. Save all utility bills (in your name), bank statements (Texas address), and any other time-stamped Texas records.

5
After 12 Months · Before Census Date

Submit the Texas Residency Questionnaire

Log in to UT Direct and complete the Residency Questionnaire for the semester in which you want reclassification. You must submit before the semester's official census date. A UT Residency Officer will review your application and may request additional documentation.

Processing time: Allow up to four weeks for review. Submit early. If the portal is full, use the UT Box widget to upload additional documents.
6
After Approval

Receive Classification & Pay In-State Rates

Once approved, you'll receive a Secure Academic Note (SAN) via MyUT confirming your reclassification. Your tuition will be updated to in-state rates for that semester forward. Note: UT may require a second check-in petition approximately one year after initial classification to confirm you've maintained residency.

Required Documents

Your Documentation Checklist

UT Austin's Office of the Registrar may request any combination of the following. The more applicable documentation you provide, the stronger your case. All documents covering the full 12-month period are essential.

Property Ownership (Domicile Proof)

Warranty Deed — listing your name, Texas address, and purchase date, filed with the State of Texas
Copy of deed showing sole or joint-marital ownership
Mortgage statements (if applicable) showing Texas address
Property tax records filed in Texas
Homestead exemption filing (if applicable)

Physical Presence (12 Months Continuous)

Utility bills (electric, gas, water, internet) — your name and Texas address for each of the 12 months
Texas bank account statements — your name and Texas address for each of the 12 months
Valid Texas Driver's License or State ID — must be issued at least 12 months before the census date
Texas voter registration card or record
Lease or rental agreement (if applicable) for 12-month period

Identity & Legal Status

U.S. Passport or birth certificate (proof of citizenship)
Permanent Resident Card (Green Card), if applicable
USCIS I-485 filing receipt (if application pending)
Valid visa documentation (eligible non-immigrant types only)
REAL ID-compliant Texas Driver's License or ID Card

For Dependent Students — Parent Docs

Parent's copy of warranty deed proving Texas residential property ownership
Parent's federal tax return showing student claimed as dependent
Parent's Texas Driver's License or ID (issued 12+ months ago)
Parent's utility bills listing Texas address — all 12 months
Parent's Texas bank statements — all 12 months

Critical Rules

What Can Disqualify You

Understanding what doesn't count is just as important as knowing what does.

🚫

Student-Status Activities Don't Count

Work-study, teaching/research assistantships, fellowships, and stipends do not qualify as gainful employment for domicile purposes.

🚫

Education-Only Presence Doesn't Count

Renting an apartment solely while enrolled — without other Texas ties — is not sufficient. Texas domicile must be independent of educational objectives.

🚫

Property Rented to Others Won't Work Alone

If the qualifying property is rented out and you don't live in it, it can't serve as your primary domicile. You must reside there (or use the LLC/business route instead).

💡

The LLC Alternative

If the student lives in dorms, a parent can own the property and operate it as an LLC rental business. The business ownership + management qualifies as a separate domicile path for a dependent student.

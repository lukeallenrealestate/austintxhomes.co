Relocating for Google · Austin TX

Where Google Austin comp actually lands you

FAANG-elite total comp puts Google Austin buyers in one of three price bands, not three lifestyle buckets. Downtown condo at $700K to $1.5M. Eanes ISD single-family at $1.5M to $4M. Central AISD single-family at $1M to $2.5M. The question is which band your seniority and family stage land in.

By Luke Allen · TREC #788149 · September 21, 2026

Talk to a realtor who has closed Google deals

The most useful lens for a Google Austin relocation buyer is not lifestyle, it is compensation. Google Austin comp reaches materially higher than a typical Apple or CrowdStrike Austin package, and that single fact opens up submarkets that stay theoretical for a comparable buyer at a lower-comp employer. Westlake and Rollingwood inside Eanes ISD is the clearest example. My Apple buyers hear "very few reach Westlake without other income" and it is true. My Google buyers hear "your comp reaches Westlake if that is where you want to be" and that is also true. The Austin decision at Google is genuinely different from the Austin decision at other tech employers because the check-writing capacity is different.

That does not mean every Google buyer should stretch into the top of their pre-approval. Most of my Google closes land inside a comfortable comp-to-home ratio, not at the ceiling. What comp does buy you is optionality. You get to pick the band that fits your life rather than the band your budget forces you into. The three bands below are where my Google buyers actually land, along with what each one buys and what it does not.

Band 1 - Downtown high-rise condo, walk or bike to 500 W 2nd

Band 1 · $700K to $1.5M · IC or newer manager

Rainey, Second Street, Seaholm high-rise condos

Fits: L4-L5 engineer, single or couple, no school-age kids yet, wants zero commute

Band 1 is the default for a Google Austin buyer whose primary variable is "walk out my door and be at the office in fifteen minutes." The 500 West Second office sits at the edge of the Second Street district, which puts Seaholm, Rainey, and the north half of the West End inside a genuinely walkable radius. Well-appointed 1 to 2 bedroom units in the newer high-rise stock land inside the $700K to $1.5M band, and the mid-floor units are the value sweet spot on a total-cost-per-year basis once HOA dues are in the math.

HOA dues are the piece Band 1 buyers most often underestimate. Well-run downtown buildings run $700 to $1,200 a month, and that number covers amenity deck, concierge, water, some utilities, and building reserves. On a $900K purchase a $1,000 HOA takes your effective monthly carry to what a $1.2M single-family would cost on paper. Not necessarily a bad trade, but it belongs in your math on day one and I walk every Band 1 Google buyer through the dues history and any pending assessment before we write an offer.

The Google-specific question I get most often on Band 1: does the office really matter enough to pay downtown pricing when I could buy in Central Austin for the same money and drive in on my one or two in-office days a week. Honest answer: for most Google Austin teams, no, and that is why Band 2 exists.

Band 2 - Central Austin AISD, walkable inner-city with a yard

Band 2 · $1M to $2.5M · Mid-senior IC or family

Tarrytown, Clarksville, Zilker, Bouldin, or Mueller

Fits: L5-L6 engineer, couple or small family, wants Central AISD and a yard, one-two day hybrid

Band 2 is the largest slice of my Google closes. It fits the buyer who has outgrown the downtown condo but still wants a walkable, bikeable Central Austin life, and whose team's in-office cadence supports a 15 to 25 minute drive in. Product runs from a 1930s Tarrytown bungalow with a renovation history to a 2020s Zilker rebuild to a Mueller new-urbanist row-lot to a Bouldin midcentury remodel. The stylistic variety is real, and the price band supports genuinely different physical products at the same dollar amount.

Which specific pocket to target inside Band 2 depends on the family stage. Tarrytown and Clarksville (78703) are AISD Casis Elementary, O.Henry Middle, and Austin High, which is a strong track for a family that wants inner-city plus solid public schools. Zilker and Bouldin (78704) have their own AISD paths and are the closest to the 500 West Second office by drive. Mueller (78723) is more spread out, has strong new-build inventory, and is the value pick if you can accept slightly less inner-city character. My default when a Band 2 buyer cannot decide is to tour one home in each of the three subpockets before we start narrowing.

Band 2 is where Google buyers most often surprise themselves. They walk in wanting a downtown condo and walk out wanting a Zilker bungalow, because the yard, the neighborhood, and the AISD track add up faster than the walkability tradeoff subtracts.

Band 3 - Eanes ISD family, the tier Apple buyers cannot reach

Band 3 · $1.5M to $4M+ · Senior IC, staff, or above

Westlake, Rollingwood, West Lake Hills inside Eanes ISD

Fits: L6 plus engineer or director, family with school-age kids, deciding variable is school district

Band 3 is the tier that separates Google Austin from every other tech employer page on this site. Apple buyers rarely reach Westlake and Tarrytown pricing without other household income. CrowdStrike buyers are bracketed lower. Senior Google engineers routinely close in the $1.5M to $3M range in Eanes ISD without stretching, because total comp at the L6-plus levels genuinely supports it. That is not a marketing claim. It is a real difference in the buyer profile I represent when the badge says Google versus when it says Apple.

Eanes ISD (Westlake, Rollingwood, West Lake Hills) is the top-performing public school district in the Austin metro and one of the top districts in Texas. Lots are bigger, streets are quieter, and the neighborhood identity leans traditional suburban rather than urban. Product runs from 1980s and 1990s ranch on a golf-course lot to newer luxury builds tucked back on quiet cul-de-sacs. Commute to the 500 West Second office runs 15 to 25 minutes off peak and 25 to 35 at peak, which is manageable for a Google engineer on a one to three day hybrid cadence but starts to feel long for a five-day-a-week in-office role.

The subtle Band 3 decision I walk Google buyers through: the difference between Westlake proper (older, more established, the "safest" resale), Rollingwood (smaller, quieter, pocket-of-perfection feel), and West Lake Hills (larger lots, hillier terrain, more architectural variety). All three are inside Eanes ISD and all three trade at broadly similar prices per square foot, but the daily neighborhood experience is meaningfully different across the three.

Comparison: why the Google Austin decision differs from the Apple one

The comp bracket, not the commute, is the variable that reshapes the map

I write both this page and the Apple Austin relocation page from first-hand deal experience. The most useful contrast for a buyer weighing where to live is that Apple compensation, as strong as it is, honestly does not reach Westlake or Tarrytown for a typical IC or manager without other household income. That is on the Apple page in exactly those words. My Apple buyers understand it and land in the northwest close-in, Steiner, or Central AISD tracks that their comp supports.

Google buyers hear a different version of the same conversation. Senior Google engineers in Austin routinely qualify for and close in the $1.5M to $3M range in Eanes ISD, and the numbers do not require a stretch to make them work. That is the compensation reality at the top FAANG comp bracket, and it changes which submarkets are genuinely available to you. If you have already read the Apple page and thought "the Westlake ceiling is not a hard rule for me", you may be right. That difference is the whole reason I keep the two employer pages separate rather than lumping them together as "big tech."

How team on-site cadence reshapes the band decision

Google Austin varies materially by team on how many days a week engineers are expected in the 500 West Second office. Rather than a single Google-wide answer, ask your specific team lead before you make the location decision. My rough framework for the buyers I represent:

  • Five days a week on-site. Band 1 or the closer parts of Band 2 (Zilker, Bouldin, close-in Tarrytown). Westlake becomes a real commute at peak, not a rounding error.
  • Three days a week hybrid. Any of the three bands work. Band 2 or Band 3 fit best because you have the flexibility to earn the 20 to 30 minute commute on the days you drive in.
  • One or two days a week. Bands 2 and 3 open fully. Band 3 (Westlake) becomes especially attractive because the commute cost is amortized across fewer trips.
  • Fully remote. Bands 2 and 3 both work, and the far Hill Country (Dripping Springs, Bee Cave, Lakeway) opens up as a genuinely reasonable option if you want acreage. I flag to fully-remote Google buyers that the far-out option often reads better on paper than it lives day-to-day once the isolation and drive to city amenities set in.

Bottom line

The Google Austin decision is a comp-band decision first and a lifestyle decision second. That is the honest reading from every Google buyer I have represented in this market. Downtown condo Band 1 is real, Central AISD Band 2 is where most of my closes land, and Eanes ISD Band 3 is genuinely available in a way that is not true for buyers at most other tech employers.

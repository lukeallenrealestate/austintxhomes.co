How This Report Is Built

This Report Uses Real ACTRIS MLS Data — Not Estimates

Most online "Barton Hills market reports" recycle year-old Zillow or Redfin averages that smooth out the neighborhood-specific story. This report is different. Every statistic below is computed directly from the live Austin Board of Realtors (ACTRIS) MLS database, filtered to the AISD Barton Hills Elementary attendance zone — the cleanest geographic definition of the Barton Hills neighborhood.

Why the school zone filter? Because "78704" covers way more than Barton Hills — it includes Bouldin, Travis Heights, Zilker, Galindo, South Lamar, and parts of South Congress. Generic 78704 averages would conflate dramatically different submarkets. The Barton Hills Elementary zone, by contrast, is tightly aligned with what locals call Barton Hills.

Luke Allen extracts these numbers from the same MLS that listing agents use to price their homes. The analysis is statistical, not anecdotal — medians and means are computed over the full population of active, pending, and closed listings, not cherry-picked comps. Quick Answer: The Barton Hills 2026 Market in One Paragraph

The State of Barton Hills in Mid-2026

The Barton Hills neighborhood in Austin Texas (AISD Barton Hills Elementary zone, primarily ZIP 78704) currently has 24 active single-family home listings with a median list price of $2,037,500 ($2,263,132 mean), median $815/sqft, and prices ranging from $925,000 to $3,950,000. There are 10 homes in escrow (7 active under contract, 3 pending). The median home has been on the market 166 days — nearly twice as long as Tarrytown — with 0 active listings under 30 days old and 63% (15 of 24) sitting 90+ days stale. 41.7% of active listings have reduced their original asking price by an average of 6.7%. The defining structural fact: 58% of active inventory is new construction built 2010 or later (12 built 2020+), making Barton Hills one of Austin's most aggressively rebuilt established neighborhoods. New construction commands $832/sqft versus $629/sqft for 1945-1979 mid-century stock — a 32% new-build premium. 88% of homes feed O. Henry Middle School and Austin High School, the same feeder pattern as Tarrytown — meaning Barton Hills offers the Austin High pipeline at a meaningfully lower entry price than the Tarrytown/Casis equivalent. Pool homes carry a 17.7% per-square-foot premium, but pools are common (58% of homes have one). Recent closed sales transacted at exactly 100% of list with median list-to-close DOM of 37.5 days. The bottom line: Barton Hills is the highest-leverage buyer's market among Austin's top-tier school zones in 2026.

Why the Barton Hills Pool Premium Is Smaller Than Tarrytown

When 58% of homes in a neighborhood have pools, the pool stops being a luxury differentiator and becomes a baseline expectation. The marginal value of a pool in Barton Hills is the difference between "yes, has pool" and "no pool" — not the difference between "rare luxury feature" and "no pool" as in Tarrytown. The structural lot economics matter too: Barton Hills's 0.252-acre median lot more easily accommodates a pool without dominating the yard, making pool installation a more natural part of typical Barton Hills design.

For sellers, this means pool installation in Barton Hills produces a smaller relative premium than in Tarrytown. For buyers, it means a no-pool home in Barton Hills is genuinely distinctive — possibly negotiating leverage with sellers worried about appealing to the dominant pool-expecting buyer pool.

Buyer Strategy

Barton Hills Buyer Offer Strategy
by Days-on-Market Cohort

The 166-day median DOM in Barton Hills creates one of Austin's most differentiated buyer-strategy environments. The right offer depends entirely on which DOM cohort a listing occupies. Generic "offer 95% of list" advice is wrong — the data show much more variance.

0–30 DAYS
Zero active listings in this cohort. No applicable strategy. The fact that no Barton Hills SFR has come to market in the last 30 days is itself a market signal: sellers are not rushing to list, and any new listing that does appear will likely face the same 166-day median environment unless priced aggressively below comparables from day one. If a fresh listing does appear at sub-market pricing, move fast — that is the only condition under which Barton Hills inventory clears quickly.
30–60 DAYS
3 active listings. Offer 92–95% of list with normal contingencies. Sellers in this cohort are starting to question their pricing but have not yet capitulated. Pull the original list price — if they've already reduced once, they are calibrating; if not, your offer puts them at the decision point. Inspection and appraisal contingencies remain standard practice here.
60–90 DAYS
6 active listings. Offer 88–93% of current list, knowing many sellers will accept 89–91% to close out 60+ days of carrying costs. This cohort is the sweet spot of Barton Hills negotiation in 2026 — long enough to give the buyer real leverage, short enough that the seller hasn't started losing hope. Build inspection and appraisal contingencies in as leverage. Pull every recent comp in the same subdivision before bidding.
90+ DAYS (STALE)
15 of 24 active listings (the dominant cohort). Offer 82–90% of current list. Carrying costs (taxes, insurance, mortgage) are eating into seller psychology after 90+ days. Many in this cohort have already reduced once at average 6.7% — pull the original list and the math may show your offer is closer to fair value than the current list suggests. Inspection findings become extremely powerful negotiation leverage. Sellers in the 120+ day cohort often accept inspection-period repair credits at 1.5-2x the cost just to close.
CASH BUYERS
Apply an additional 3–5% discount on top of the DOM-based price. Cash carries real value in Barton Hills, where appraisal risk for new construction is real and the typical buyer is financed and rate-sensitive. Cash plus 14-day close plus no contingencies can close transactions at 12–15% below list on stale inventory. The cash buyer in the $1.5M–$3M tier has more leverage in Barton Hills than in any other comparable Austin school zone.
NEW BUILD VS ORIGINAL CONDITION
Apply different negotiation strategies based on era. Original-condition 1945-1979 stock is moving in 30-40 days at list price — for these listings, offer at or near list with strong terms. New-construction 2020+ inventory is the stale cohort — for these listings, the 82-90% strategy applies. The era of the home is as important a negotiation variable as the DOM. UNDERCUT THE TIER

NEW CONSTRUCTION SPECIFICALLY

Sellers who anchor on aspirational new-build pricing are joining a multi-month stall.
USE THE AUSTIN HIGH ANGLE
Lead marketing with the Austin High feeder pattern. 88% of Barton Hills feeds O. Henry Middle and Austin High — the same prestigious AISD pipeline as Tarrytown. Many buyers searching Tarrytown for school feeder reasons would happily accept Barton Hills at a $300K lower median entry. Your listing should explicitly call out the school pipeline. Listing late February through early June puts your property in front of the highest-intent buyer pool. September-early October is a secondary window before the holiday slowdown. Avoid mid-November through mid-January — the family buyer cohort doesn't shop.

What Drives Barton Hills
Home Prices

Five structural factors drive Barton Hills pricing — the same factors that create both the school-zone premium and the current new-construction oversupply.

01
Austin High Feeder — The Hidden School-Zone Story
88% of Barton Hills feeds O. Henry Middle School and Austin High School — the same pipeline as Tarrytown's Casis zone. This is the single most underappreciated value driver in Barton Hills. Buyers and relocation specialists routinely default to Tarrytown for the Austin High feed, paying $300K-$500K more for what is effectively the same middle/high school experience from grade 6 onward. Barton Hills sits immediately west and south of Zilker Park, home to Austin City Limits Festival and Barton Springs Pool. The walk-to-Zilker premium is real — homes within a 10-15 minute walk of Zilker command price premiums of 8-15% versus comparable homes deeper into Barton Hills. Barton Springs Pool itself is a quality-of-life amenity that genuinely affects buyer decision-making. The Greenbelt at Barton Creek provides hiking and biking access. Together these create the South Austin lifestyle case that drives a different demographic of buyer than Tarrytown's bluff-and-trees positioning attracts.
03
The New Construction Wave — 58% of Active Inventory
The dominant structural fact about Barton Hills today is that more than half the active SFR inventory is new construction built 2010 or later. This is dramatically higher than Tarrytown's 31% new-build share and reshapes nearly every market dynamic. Luke Allen advises buyers and sellers to understand which sub-market they are entering (new vs original) before pricing.
04

What's the best offer strategy for a Barton Hills buyer?
Tier the offer to DOM cohort: 30-60 days = 92-95% of list. 60-90 days = 88-93%. 90+ days (15 of 24 active) = 82-90%. Cash buyers apply 3-5% additional discount. Original-condition mid-century homes that hit market fresh need close-to-list offers with strong terms — they move in 30-40 days.
How should I price my Barton Hills home as a seller?
Anchor on closed $/sqft ($746 median), not active list $/sqft ($815). Adjust up for new build (+32%), pool (+17.7%), or premium subdivision (Sec 01, West Park, Ridgewood). List 3-5% below CMA target to be the obvious value pick. Avoid the 10-of-24 "reduce later" trap.
What's the difference between Barton Hills and Zilker?

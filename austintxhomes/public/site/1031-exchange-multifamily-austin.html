Case one

A Bay Area SFR rolled into an East Austin duplex.

Marcus owned a 1,400-square-foot single-family rental in Oakland that he purchased in 2014 for $585,000. By March of 2026 he sold it for $1,180,000. His basis after depreciation sat at roughly $490,000, which put his deferred federal gain and depreciation recapture obligation near $690,000. Without a 1031, that tax bill would have landed close to $190,000 in his next return.

Marcus's Qualified Intermediary took constructive receipt of the proceeds on April 3, the day the Oakland sale funded. From that moment, he had until May 18 to deliver a written list of replacement properties and until September 30 to close on one of them. He engaged Luke on April 7. Four days to catch up on a market he had never worked in.

Luke spent the first week pulling every East Austin duplex priced between $900,000 and $1,250,000 that had been on the market long enough to negotiate. Nine properties survived the first cut. Marcus flew in on April 15 for a two-day tour. By April 22 they had identified three properties under the 3-Property Rule and submitted an offer on a 2008-built duplex in 78721, close to the Mueller submarket, listed at $1,095,000. It closed on June 12 at $1,035,000, well inside the 180-day window.

Marcus rolled every dollar of Oakland equity into the duplex, took on slightly more debt to meet the equal-or-greater requirement, and avoided the $190,000 tax bill completely. The duplex rents both sides at a combined $5,400 per month. The California Franchise Tax Board filed a Form 3840 reminder in the fall, which his CPA responded to.

Case two

A Denver condo portfolio consolidated into a Pflugerville fourplex.

Priya owned three Denver condos she had accumulated between 2017 and 2020. Managing them across two neighborhoods had become the kind of drag that eats returns. In July of 2026 she sold all three in the same week for a combined $1,620,000 against an aggregate basis near $810,000. She wanted to consolidate into one property with professional management.

Her QI held the aggregated proceeds. July 29 became day zero. September 12 was her identification deadline. January 25, 2027 was her close deadline.

Austin fourplexes in the $1.4M to $1.8M range are a thin market. Luke found eleven candidates. Priya identified five under the 200% Rule because the combined asking prices sat at $7.9 million, well inside the 200% ceiling against her relinquished $1.62M. One, a 2015-built fourplex in Pflugerville with an in-place property manager and a $9,800 per month rent roll, closed on November 4 at $1,595,000.

Priya's deferred federal gain was approximately $810,000. Deferring it saved her roughly $225,000 in combined capital gains and depreciation recapture. Colorado also required its own continuing-interest filing because the sold property sat in a state income tax jurisdiction.

The common thread

Why both investors chose small multifamily over SFR.

Both Marcus and Priya held single-family rentals before the exchange. Both rolled into small multifamily. The reason in both cases was the same: operational concentration. One roof, one driveway, one HVAC system per unit, and one tenant lease signing per year per door, instead of one of each per standalone house. The economics on cap rate were actually tighter on the multifamily side than on comparable SFR. What won was management overhead per rent dollar collected.

Austin small multifamily (two to four units) also sits in a residential loan bracket rather than a commercial one. That meant Marcus could use a 25-year amortization with a conventional investor loan, not a 20-year commercial product with a five-year balloon. Priya's fourplex qualified for the same residential underwriting because four units is the upper boundary.

The underwriting question

Underwriting Austin multifamily for a 1031 buyer.

A 1031 buyer underwrites differently than a cash buyer. The deferred gain creates a basis that follows the buyer into the next property, which affects depreciation downstream. Both Marcus and Priya had their CPAs run basis carryover calculations before the identification deadline so they knew the depreciation schedule on the replacement property before they committed.

Rent comparables matter more than list price for multifamily. Luke pulls rent rolls on recent duplex and fourplex sales in the target submarket so his 1031 clients see what the actual per-door income is, not what the listing broker implies. A $1.1 million East Austin duplex that rents at $5,400 produces different numbers than one that rents at $4,100, and the price alone will not tell you which is which.

Most California buyers assume the no-income-tax Texas savings easily cover higher property tax. Numbers are directional, not advice; always confirm with a CPA.

What is the property tax difference between California and Texas?

California property tax is roughly 1.1 percent of assessed value (Prop 13 locked). Texas has no state income tax but offsets with higher property tax, roughly 1.8 to 2.3 percent of market value in Travis County and around 2.0 percent in Williamson County. On a $750,000 Austin home the annual tax is roughly $16,000 before homestead exemption. The homestead exemption plus the 10 percent appraisal cap meaningfully reduce the effective rate for primary residences held more than a year.

A typical conventional loan closes in 30 to 35 days from accepted offer. Cash offers close in as little as 14 days. The 60 to 90 day California to Austin relocation window is usually: weeks 1 to 2 are intake and remote tours, week 3 is offer, weeks 4 to 7 is inspection, appraisal, and underwriting, week 8 is close and mobile notary, weeks 9 to 12 is move coordination.

Which Austin neighborhoods do California transplants pick?

The most common picks fall into four groups. Urban central: Mueller, East Austin, Hyde Park, Crestview, Clarksville. Family suburbs with top schools: Westlake, Lake Travis ISD, Eanes ISD, Dripping Springs. Value-per-square-foot suburbs: Round Rock, Pflugerville, Leander, Cedar Park. Luxury and waterfront: Westlake Hills, Rollingwood, Lake Austin, Lakeway. California buyers from LA and Orange County tend to target Westlake and Lake Travis; San Francisco and San Jose buyers tend to target Mueller, East Austin, and Clarksville for the walkable urban feel.

What are the hidden costs of moving from California to Austin?

The four hidden costs most California buyers underestimate: higher Texas property tax (offsets the state income tax savings in year one), HOA dues on most newer Austin neighborhoods ($40 to $400 per month), higher homeowners insurance due to Texas hail and wind exposure ($150 to $300 per month on a $750K home), and higher cooling costs in summer. The Texas no-income-tax savings usually still net out positive for California transplants earning over $200,000.

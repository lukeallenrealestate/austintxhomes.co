The housing cost math, with real numbers

Dallas metro median single-family home sits around $410,000 in 2026. Austin metro median sits around $545,000. On a comparable home in a comparable neighborhood, the Dallas purchase will typically cost $100,000 to $200,000 less than the Austin equivalent. For a buyer whose primary filter is maximum house per dollar, Dallas wins straightforwardly.

Where this reverses is at the top of the market. Highland Park and Preston Hollow luxury pricing in Dallas can match or exceed Westlake and Tarrytown pricing in Austin. The specific submarkets at $3M-plus are closer to even, with each metro winning in different pockets. For most buyers at $400,000 to $1,500,000, Dallas is cheaper. Above $3,000,000, the comparison is closer to a wash.

The tech career depth, with real employer context

Austin's tech employer base in 2026 includes Tesla's Gigafactory in southeast Austin, Apple's $1 billion Parmer Lane campus, Google's downtown Austin office at 500 W Second, Oracle's $1.2 billion Lakeshore headquarters that moved from Redwood City in 2020, Samsung's $17 billion Taylor semiconductor fab, Meta Austin, AMD, CrowdStrike, Indeed, and dozens of mid-sized tech employers plus a deep startup ecosystem. For a software engineer, product manager, data scientist, or technical founder, the depth of employer options and the network density of the Austin tech community is materially greater than Dallas.

Dallas has strong technology employment through AT&T headquarters, Texas Instruments, Match Group, and the regional offices of several big tech companies. For telecom, enterprise IT, data center infrastructure, and traditional-corporate-IT careers, Dallas is competitive or better than Austin. For FAANG-adjacent and venture-funded startup careers, Austin has clear advantage.

The schools question, honestly

Dallas has three nationally ranked suburban public school districts with large footprints: Highland Park ISD (small enclave, exceptional performance), Plano ISD (large footprint, consistent strength), and Frisco ISD (growing footprint, strong). These districts cover a meaningful portion of the Dallas metro and give families multiple viable submarket options at various price points.

Austin's top-tier public districts are Eanes ISD (Westlake), Lake Travis ISD (Lakeway, Spicewood), and Round Rock ISD (Cedar Park, parts of Round Rock). All three are strong, but they are geographically concentrated. Eanes specifically is a small district with limited geographic footprint, which means the housing-cost premium to buy into it is meaningful. Dallas offers more top-tier public school options across more submarkets at a wider price range.

Austin ISD has specific strengths at Casis Elementary, Bryker Woods, and the LASA magnet high school, but AISD overall is more variable in performance than the top Dallas suburban districts. Private school options are competitive in both metros.

The daily life comparison that no spreadsheet captures

Austin's daily life skews outdoor. Barton Springs Pool, Lady Bird Lake trail (the 10-mile loop around downtown), Lake Austin boating, Lake Travis weekend trips, Hill Country hiking at 30 minutes west. The live music scene is unmatched in Texas and genuinely one of the best in the US (SXSW in March, ACL in October, year-round venues from the small Continental Club to large Moody Center). Food culture skews BBQ, Tex-Mex, food trucks, and independent restaurants. The city feels younger and more casual.

Dallas's daily life skews cosmopolitan. NorthPark Center and Highland Park Village retail. Dallas Symphony, Dallas Opera, Broadway touring at the AT&T Performing Arts Center. Four major-league pro teams at major-market scale (Cowboys, Mavericks, Stars, Rangers). Upscale dining and cocktail scene with meaningful depth. The city feels more established and more corporate. For buyers who value upscale retail, pro sports, and performing arts, Dallas delivers at a scale Austin does not.

The simplest version: Austin feels like a music festival that became a city. Dallas feels like a business that became a city. Both work for different people.

The flight connectivity advantage Dallas has

DFW is a major American Airlines hub with direct flights to Europe, Asia, Latin America, and essentially every major US city. For frequent business travelers, families with coastal relatives, or households that fly internationally more than a few times a year, this is a tangible quality-of-life advantage that compounds.

Austin-Bergstrom International is a single-terminal regional airport that has grown substantially in the last decade and now offers direct flights to a reasonable set of US and some European destinations. For a buyer whose air travel is mostly domestic business trips to major US cities, Austin works fine. For international travelers or anyone who needs direct flights to secondary cities, DFW's connectivity is a real advantage.

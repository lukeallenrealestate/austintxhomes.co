The three license types that define the deal

Austin STR runs on three license types

The City of Austin regulates short-term rentals through a type classification framework. Each type has distinct application requirements, operating conditions, and buyer-side implications. Confirm current city code and consult counsel before closing.

01
Owner-occupied

Type 1 STR

Owner lives on the property and rents out rooms, a secondary unit, or an accessory dwelling unit (ADU). The most durable Austin STR category through all regulatory cycles. Typical use: primary-residence buyer who wants an income supplement from an ADU, garage apartment, or spare rooms.

Buyer profile:Primary-residence buyer who wants STR income from an ADU or portion of the main structure.
02
Non-owner-occupied residential

Type 2 STR

Investor-owned STR in single-family or duplex residential properties where the owner does not live on-site. Extended regulatory and court-litigation history in Austin starting 2016 with evolving operating framework through recent years. Current enforcement landscape should be verified at the time of purchase for the specific property address.

Buyer profile:Dedicated STR investor acquiring a property for non-owner-occupied operation. Highest regulatory sensitivity, highest return potential at specific addresses.
03
Multifamily

Type 3 STR

STR within multifamily properties (apartment buildings, condo buildings) subject to building rules and condo HOA restrictions in addition to city code. Downtown Austin high-rise condos frequently have building-level STR restrictions that are more restrictive than city code.

Buyer profile:Investor acquiring a condo unit specifically for STR, where building HOA permissions matter as much as city regulations.

Hill Country ETJ

Hill Country wedding, wine-trail, and weekend-getaway STR demand. Historically a friendlier regulatory environment than Austin proper for non-owner-occupied STR. Higher per-night rates during peak wedding season (March-October). Larger lots support bachelorette and family-group STR formats.

Submarket 4
Hays County

Wimberley 78676

Established vacation-rental tourism market anchored by the Blue Hole, Jacob's Well, and the Hill Country river tubing corridor. Mature STR operating infrastructure (cleaners, property managers, local service providers). Different county regulatory environment than Austin.

Submarket 5
Spicewood + Lake Travis

Lake Travis vacation 78669

Lake-access vacation STR with Hill Country character. Higher acquisition costs offset by premium lake-view and waterfront nightly rates. Peak seasons: summer boat months plus Austin event weekends. HOA restrictions vary significantly by subdivision; waterfront STR often faces LCRA permit considerations in addition to STR rules.

373 active 78669 listings between $500K and $2M averaging $1.06M.
Submarket Bonus
Commercial or mixed-use

Austin commercial-zoned STR

For dedicated STR investors prioritizing regulatory certainty: commercial-zoned or mixed-use properties generally face fewer residential-STR restrictions than single-family or duplex residential properties. Trades regulatory simplicity for higher per-unit acquisition cost and often multifamily operating complexity.

Why the submarket decision comes before the specific property

Austin STR investment buyers routinely fall into one of two traps. The first: identifying a specific property first, then trying to verify STR permissions after making an offer, only to discover HOA restrictions or zoning constraints that make the operating plan unworkable. The second: assuming a submarket-wide regulatory posture based on general Austin STR coverage, then discovering block-by-block or building-by-building variation that invalidates the general assumption.

The right sequence runs the other direction. First confirm STR strategy (owner-occupied Type 1, non-owner-occupied Type 2 in residential, or Type 3 in multifamily). Second match the strategy to the regulatory environment of the specific submarket (Austin proper vs Dripping Springs ETJ vs Wimberley vs Lake Travis). Third narrow to specific neighborhoods within that submarket where the regulatory posture matches the strategy. Only then evaluate specific properties.

What are the Austin short-term rental license types?

Austin regulates STR through a type-based framework. Type 1 covers owner-occupied STR where the owner lives on-property. Type 2 covers non-owner-occupied STR in single-family or duplex residential (extended regulatory history). Type 3 covers STR in multifamily properties. Each has distinct application requirements, operating conditions, and compliance obligations including Hotel Occupancy Tax (HOT) registration.

Are short-term rentals legal in Austin?

Regulated, not uniformly banned. Owner-occupied Type 1 has been consistently permitted. Non-owner-occupied Type 2 in residential zones has an extended regulatory history with court rulings affecting enforcement. Commercial and mixed-use STR generally faces fewer restrictions than residential. Verify current city code for the specific property address before closing.

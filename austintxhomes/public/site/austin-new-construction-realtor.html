Who works for whom in a new construction transaction

Builder sales rep vs. your buyer-side Realtor

The structural conflict of interest in walking into a builder sales center alone. Both of these roles exist in every new build transaction. One is on your side.

Builder sales rep

On the builder's side

Employed by the builder, paid by the builder, bonused on builder revenue. Trained to minimize incentive concessions, maximize upgrade sales, and route financing through the preferred lender. Many are not even licensed Realtors.

  • Discourages buyer-side representation after first visit
  • Markets upgrades at full retail, not negotiated pricing
  • Steers to preferred lender regardless of rate
  • Pushes contract clauses that favor builder
Your buyer-side Realtor

On your side, builder-paid

Licensed under TREC with fiduciary duty to you. Compensation comes from the builder's buyer-agent commission (budgeted separately from price). Zero out-of-pocket cost to the buyer. Represents your interest through the full transaction.

  • Negotiates incentive stack and upgrade pricing in writing
  • Runs preferred-lender-vs-independent-lender math
  • Reviews contract clauses before signing
  • Coordinates independent inspection access
The three Austin builder tiers

Three Austin builder tiers

Each tier operates with different scale, different design quality, different incentive structure, and different contract terms. The right tier for any specific buyer depends on budget, submarket priority, and design preference.

I
Production tier

Volume production builders

Austin's largest new-build volume. Operate in production-scale master-planned subdivisions in the outer metro. Shorter construction timelines (6-10 months typical). Larger standing inventory of completed homes. Most aggressive incentive stacks, particularly on standing inventory the builder needs to move.

Primary builders:Pulte Homes, Taylor Morrison, DR Horton, Lennar, KB Home, Meritage Homes, Perry Homes, Centex
II
Mid-tier

Mid-tier design-forward builders

Smaller community footprints than production builders. Higher design quality, more floor plan options, better finish levels at base price. Longer construction timelines (10-14 months typical). Less aggressive standing inventory but better negotiation on upgrade allowance and design-studio pricing.

Primary builders:David Weekley Homes, Highland Homes, Village Builders by Lennar, Trendmaker
III
Semi-custom + luxury

Semi-custom and luxury builders

Semi-custom build on buyer-selected lots or in boutique developer communities. Higher base price. Construction timelines 12-18 months. Different negotiation levers: lot premium, custom plan modifications, upgraded structural options, extended warranty.

Primary builders:Toll Brothers, Drees Custom Homes, Scott Felder Homes, Monticello Homes, Partners in Building, local custom builders

New construction FAQ

Common Austin new construction questions

Do I need a realtor to buy new construction in Austin?

Not required, but materially beneficial. Three reasons: builder sales reps work for the builder (not you); buyer representation is paid by the builder (free to you); new construction contracts, incentive stacks, and preferred-lender terms are substantially more complex than resale. Luke Allen (TREC #788149) represents Austin new construction buyers across the full builder landscape.

Who are the main new construction builders in the Austin area?

Three tiers. Production (Pulte, Taylor Morrison, DR Horton, Lennar, KB Home, Meritage, Perry, Centex) in Pflugerville, Hutto, Kyle, Manor, Elgin, Round Rock, outer metro. Mid-tier (David Weekley, Highland, Village Builders, Trendmaker) with higher design quality. Semi-custom and luxury (Toll Brothers, Drees, Scott Felder, Monticello, local custom) in Dripping Springs, Spicewood, Leander, Cedar Park, Hill Country.

What are the negotiation levers on an Austin new construction purchase?

Three groups totaling 10 levers. Financial package: closing credits, preferred-lender rate buydown, base price (least flexible). Spec package: design-studio upgrade allowance, appliance and landscape inclusions. Transaction structure: title company choice (Texas buyer right), independent inspection access, HOA transfer waiver, extended rate lock on to-be-built. The dedicated playbook page runs each lever.

What is the preferred lender incentive and when does it hurt the buyer?

Package of financial inducements ($5K-$20K closing cost credits or rate buydown) tied to using the builder's affiliated lender. Beneficial when the preferred lender rate is competitive with the broader market. Hurts when the preferred lender rate runs 0.25-0.75 percent above market, eroding or exceeding the incentive value over amortization. Pre-contract math on preferred-vs-independent lender rates plus incentive is critical.

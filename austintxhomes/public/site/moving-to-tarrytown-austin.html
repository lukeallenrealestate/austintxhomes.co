Why Relocation Buyers Pick Tarrytown

The Neighborhood Austin Newcomers
Choose First

If you have been researching Austin from another city, you have almost certainly landed on Tarrytown. It is the neighborhood that shows up in every "best central Austin" list, every relocation forum, every conversation with a friend who moved to Austin five years ago. The reason is simple: Tarrytown is what people picture when they imagine a walkable, tree-lined, top-schools, close-to-downtown Austin neighborhood — and unlike many "best neighborhood" answers, Tarrytown actually delivers on the promise.

Roughly 40% of Tarrytown buyers arrive from California, New York, Washington DC, Chicago, and other high-cost coastal or northern markets. They arrive with a specific set of expectations shaped by the neighborhoods they are leaving behind: mature street trees, walkable to a pool or park, real character in the housing stock, a school their kids can walk to. Tarrytown meets every one of those expectations, at a fraction of what those buyers were paying in Palo Alto or Brooklyn Heights or Georgetown.

That familiarity is a double-edged sword. Relocation buyers get comfortable with Tarrytown quickly because it feels like home. But Tarrytown has real complexity underneath the aesthetic — school-boundary variance, foundation dynamics on expansive clay soils, Heritage Tree constraints, block-by-block pricing differences, flood-zone pockets — that require an agent who actually works this neighborhood, not a generalist who happens to cover Austin. Luke Allen specializes in Tarrytown and works primarily with relocation buyers.

Common Questions

Relocating to Tarrytown,
FAQ

Can I actually buy a Tarrytown home sight-unseen?
Yes — Luke Allen closes multiple Tarrytown deals per year for buyers who have never set foot on the property. Video walkthroughs plus detailed comps plus a trusted inspection team make it possible. If you would rather see it in person, Luke coordinates one intensive 48-hour visit that covers every serious contender.
How much home does $1.5M buy in Tarrytown?
Roughly a 2,400 to 3,200 square foot home on a 7,000 to 9,000 square foot lot, likely renovated but not brand-new, in a solid Tarrytown sub-area but not the top of Upper Tarrytown. Move to $2M and you cross into the tier of renovated character homes on larger lots or new construction on smaller lots. As always, block matters more than average.
Is Casis Elementary really worth the school-zone premium?
For families with young kids: often yes. Casis is consistently one of the top-rated public elementaries in Austin ISD, and the walk-to-school ability is real for many Tarrytown blocks. But you must verify the specific address feeds into Casis before you offer — some 78703 addresses feed into Matthews Elementary instead. Luke Allen confirms this on every Tarrytown property.
What about Eanes ISD?
Western Tarrytown parcels near Westlake technically fall within Eanes ISD, which is one of the highest-rated districts in Texas. It is a small pocket but a real one, and worth understanding if you are shopping the westernmost edge of the neighborhood.
Do I need a Texas driver's license before closing?
No. You need identification and proof of funds. You will update your license after you move. Homestead exemption paperwork happens after you take title.
How does property tax work compared to my current state?
Texas has no state income tax but property tax rates are meaningfully higher than in most coastal states. For Tarrytown, budget roughly 1.9% to 2.2% of assessed value annually. The homestead exemption reduces the assessed value materially once you file — do this in January after your first full calendar year. Luke Allen walks every relocation client through the math.
Is Tarrytown safe?
Tarrytown is consistently one of the safest neighborhoods in central Austin. It is residential, quiet, and closely watched by neighbors. Typical urban precautions apply, but the crime rate is materially below city average.

Lake Austin and Lake Travis are not the same lake

The single most important waterfront decision is which lake. These two reservoirs differ structurally in ways that materially change daily use, dock design, and long-term property value. Side-by-side below.

Lake Austin

Constant-level river reservoir
Water level
Held at approximately 492.8 feet by Tom Miller Dam. Does not meaningfully fluctuate.
Length
About 21 miles from Mansfield Dam to Tom Miller Dam.
Character
Narrow river reservoir. Urban-adjacent. Residential on both shores.
Boat use
No-wake zones throughout most of the lake. Not a motorsports lake.

Fixed docks work (water depth stays stable). Boathouses common.
Submarket
Westlake 78746, Davenport Ranch, Steiner Ranch, River Place, Rob Roy.
Price range

Open-water motorsports, wakeboarding, sailing, marinas. Hill Country lake life.

Floating docks required. Dock elevation and ramp length matter.

Lakeway 78738, Rough Hollow, Spicewood 78669, Jonestown, Volente.

The five waterfront-specific due diligence steps

The waterfront protocol Luke runs on every buyer transaction

Standard residential due diligence does not cover these five items. Each one has killed otherwise-good waterfront deals in the Austin market. Run them before contract, not after.

1

LCRA shoreline permit verification

Pull the current LCRA permit record for every structure on the shoreline: boat dock, boathouse, boat lift, retaining wall, bulkhead, stairs. Non-permitted structures become the buyer's compliance problem at closing. Expired permits require renewal fees. Permit transfer paperwork must be filed at closing.

2

Water-level history against lot elevation

Lake Travis specific: pull the LCRA water-level history for the past 10 years and overlay the specific lot elevation and dock elevation. A dock that works at 672 feet is unusable at 640 feet. Buyers from constant-level markets routinely underestimate this. The analysis sits in a single chart that takes 15 minutes to produce and saves six-figure regret.

3

Physical dock and boat lift inspection

Engage a qualified lake-dock contractor to inspect the dock structure, boat lift mechanics, electrical, flotation, and attachment points. Floating docks, cantilever docks, and boathouse docks each have different failure patterns. Boat lift capacity must match the boat the buyer actually intends to keep there.

4

Shoreline access from the home

Steep lots require stair, tram, or elevator infrastructure between the home and the dock. Verify current condition, replacement cost, and HOA restrictions on tram or elevator installation. On Lake Travis specifically, lots with 150 feet of vertical from home to shoreline are routine. The access infrastructure can be a $200K-$500K separate asset.

5

Shoreline easement and erosion history

Review the lot survey against the LCRA easement line to understand the private-vs-public portion of the shoreline. Pull available erosion history (older Lake Austin and Lake Travis lots have documented shoreline recession). Verify any bulkhead or retaining-wall structural condition and permit status.

Why waterfront representation is different

A standard Austin residential transaction has a familiar due-diligence pattern: inspection, foundation, roof, HVAC, pool if applicable, HOA document review, title and survey. A waterfront transaction has that same pattern plus five additional items that do not exist in the non-waterfront market: LCRA shoreline permit review, water-level history analysis against lot elevation (Lake Travis specifically), physical dock and boat lift inspection, shoreline access infrastructure assessment, and shoreline easement review with erosion history.

Each of these additional items has killed otherwise-good waterfront deals in the Austin market. A buyer who closes on a Lake Travis property without checking the lot elevation against drought-period water levels can wake up to find the dock sitting aground after a dry summer. A buyer who closes without pulling the LCRA permit record can inherit a non-compliant dock the LCRA eventually requires them to remove at six-figure cost. A buyer who closes without inspecting the boat lift can inherit a lift that cannot handle the boat they planned to buy.

Waterfront representation is not just luxury representation applied to a waterfront lot. It is a different protocol. Luke Allen's waterfront buyer transactions run the five-step protocol above as standard procedure, every time, before contract.

What is the difference between Lake Austin and Lake Travis waterfront?

Lake Austin is a constant-level river reservoir held at approximately 492.8 feet by Tom Miller Dam, which keeps docks and shoreline infrastructure usable year-round. Lake Travis is a flood-control reservoir with full-pool elevation 681 feet that routinely sits 10-40 feet lower during Hill Country drought, affecting dock usability and shoreline visibility. The two lakes serve different buyer profiles: Lake Austin for the urban-adjacent predictable-dock buyer, Lake Travis for the Hill Country recreation and open-water boat use buyer.

What LCRA permits should I verify before buying a Lake Austin or Lake Travis waterfront home?

Verify the following before closing: boat dock permit (every private dock requires a current LCRA permit), boat lift permit, retaining wall or bulkhead permit if applicable, marina or group-dock membership documentation if applicable, and the shoreline easement defining the private-vs-public shoreline portion. Lake Austin lots additionally fall under City of Austin and Travis County shoreline rules. Non-permitted structures become the buyer's compliance problem at closing.

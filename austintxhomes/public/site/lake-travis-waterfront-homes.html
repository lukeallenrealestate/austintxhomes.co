Why Lake Travis water level is the first buyer question

Buyers moving to Lake Travis from constant-level waterfront markets (Lake Austin, Lake Minnetonka, East Coast coastal waterfront) consistently underestimate how significantly the Lake Travis water level moves. Full pool is 681 feet above mean sea level. The lake has sat at 614 feet during severe drought and at full-plus-flood 710 feet during major Hill Country rain events. The 50-year range of 100+ feet of vertical movement is a feature of the reservoir, not an anomaly.

For waterfront buyers, this translates to specific lot and dock considerations. A dock that functions at 672 feet (near full) sits aground at 630 feet (drought). A shoreline that reads as water-adjacent at 670 feet reveals 50 feet of exposed shoreline at 625 feet. A cove that is navigable at 665 feet becomes impassable at 635 feet. These are not hypothetical scenarios: Lake Travis has reached each of these levels within recent decades.

The buyer-side implication is that lot elevation matters as much as lot location. A $4M home with a 15-foot vertical drop from building pad to the water line is a different asset than a $4M home with a 150-foot vertical drop and a tram infrastructure to reach the dock. Both can be waterfront. One works at low-water levels, the other has access issues. The pre-contract analysis must include the specific lot elevation plotted against LCRA's historical water-level data for the exact lot location.

What buyer representation looks like on Lake Travis

Luke Allen's Lake Travis buyer representation runs a specific protocol on every transaction before contract. First, pull the current LCRA permit record for every shoreline structure on the lot (dock, boathouse, retaining wall, boat lift, stairs). Second, pull the LCRA hydromet 10-year water-level history and plot it against the specific lot elevation and dock pad elevation. Third, physical dock inspection by a qualified Lake Travis dock contractor including flotation, boat lift, electrical, and attachment. Fourth, shoreline access review from building pad to dock including stair or tram infrastructure condition. Fifth, HOA and deed restriction review for dock-related restrictions (dock size caps, boathouse restrictions, slip transfer rules).

These five checks run as standard procedure, not as upcharges. Each has at some point killed what would otherwise have been a successful Lake Travis purchase. The upfront investment of two to three weeks of pre-contract due diligence saves six-figure post-closing regret.

Lake Travis buyer FAQ

Common Lake Travis waterfront questions

How much is a Lake Travis waterfront home?

Lake Travis waterfront pricing starts around $1.5 million and extends to approximately $17 million at the current trophy tier. Currently 387 active listings in Lakeway and Spicewood above $1.5 million averaging $2.98 million. Pricing varies by specific shoreline position, lot elevation, dock quality, permit status, and low-water usability. True waterfront with a usable dock at drought-level water commands a meaningful premium over waterfront with elevation or dock issues.

What is the current water level on Lake Travis and does it affect property value?

Full pool is 681 feet above mean sea level. Routinely sits 10-40 feet below full during Hill Country drought. Historical minimum below 620 feet. Water level affects value in three ways: dock usability at low levels, shoreline visibility from the home, and boat access to open water from shallow coves. Pull current level from LCRA hydromet and overlay against lot elevation before any Lake Travis purchase.

Which Lake Travis community is best for waterfront?

Depends on priority. Deepest water and open-lake access: Rough Hollow 78738 and Flintrock Falls. Hill Country lower-density character: Spicewood 78669 (Barton Creek Lakeside, The Waters, Travis Settlement). Resort-amenity waterfront: Lakeway 78734 (The Hills, Costa Bella). Undeveloped rural waterfront: Point Venture and Jonestown. Large-lot deep-water yacht-tier: Spicewood west-shore inventory. Restaurant and marina walkable: Lakeway marina corridor.

What are the public boat ramps and marinas on Lake Travis?

LCRA public access includes Mansfield Dam Park, Hippie Hollow Park, Pace Bend Park (1,520 acres), Shaffer Bend, Muleshoe Bend, Grelle, and Cypress Creek. Primary private marinas: Lakeway Marina, VIP Marina, Just For Fun Marina (Volente), Hurst Harbor, Sail and Ski Center, Point Venture Marina, Jonestown Northshore. Slip availability and waitlist status matter for waterfront buyers whose boats exceed private-dock capacity.

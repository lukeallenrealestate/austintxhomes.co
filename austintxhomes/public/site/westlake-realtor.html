Westlake, bought with the pod map in hand

Westlake is not one neighborhood. It is six distinct subcommunities (Rob Roy, Davenport Ranch, Barton Creek, Lost Creek, Eanes, Toro Canyon) sharing Eanes ISD and the same ZIP, each with different lot character, country-club anchors, and buyer-type matches. Knowing which pod fits your household is the real work. The $1.6M to $9.9M price span is the surface.

Who Westlake typically pulls

Westlake inbound buyers break into three primary categories based on origin and context. First, out-of-state state-funnel inbound: California (Bay Area tech wealth, Southern California entertainment and legal), New York (Westchester, Long Island, Upper East Side), New Jersey (Essex County wealth belt), Massachusetts (Wellesley, Weston, Dover), Illinois (North Shore), Washington (Mercer Island, Medina), Minnesota (Lake Minnetonka). The common pattern: selling a long-held primary residence in a top-tier origin suburb and purchasing a comparable or upgraded Westlake home with meaningful cash down.

Second, Austin-internal relocation from other Austin submarkets: families whose kids are approaching Eanes ISD elementary age moving from Tarrytown, Mueller, Northwest Hills, or East Austin to secure the school district before kindergarten enrollment. This is a common pattern for families who prioritized central Austin walkability during their early childless years and are now prioritizing school district and larger lot size.

Third, tech-wealth and founder-exit transactions: software engineers at Tesla, Apple, Google, Oracle, and major Austin tech employers whose compensation at senior IC and leadership levels supports Westlake pricing, plus founders exiting Austin-area companies and buying into Westlake as the long-hold family home.

The pod-match logic in practice

When a prospective buyer tells me they want to buy in Westlake, the first conversation is almost always about which pod rather than price band or square footage. The reason: the pods are functionally different neighborhoods sharing a school district and ZIP code, and matching a household to the right pod matters more than finding a specific list price match.

Rob Roy matches buyers who have had waterfront as a feature of their origin home and consider it a non-negotiable. Lake Austin waterfront is categorically different from inland Westlake lots, and buyers moving from Lake Minnetonka, Lake Washington, coastal Rhode Island or similar origins rarely switch to inland after seeing Rob Roy inventory. The price premium runs 2-3x comparable inland Westlake square footage.

Davenport Ranch matches buyers who want country-club residential pattern as a lifestyle anchor (Austin Country Club proximity, resident golf and tennis cadence, country-club social density). The housing stock mix (1990s and 2000s mainstream, some newer custom) and lot sizes (typically half-acre to one-acre) read as mainstream top-tier suburban residential.

Barton Creek matches golf-centric buyers specifically. The Barton Creek Country Club golf package (72 holes across four Fazio and Palmer and Crenshaw courses) is the strongest golf offering in central Texas, and golf-prioritizing buyers who are also looking at Shoal Creek (Alabama) or Pinehurst (NC) often choose Barton Creek. The 78735 pocket adds inventory with slightly lower price per square foot than core 78746.

Lost Creek matches buyers who want canyon privacy over social density. The topography is more aggressive than Davenport Ranch (meaningful elevation changes, canyon lots, significant tree canopy), and the country club culture is lower-key than Austin CC or Barton Creek CC. Buyers who want Westlake without the country-club visibility often prefer Lost Creek.

Eanes village matches buyers prioritizing walkable village feel and shortest commute to Westlake High School. The housing stock is older on average than other pods, lots are smaller, and the price per square foot can be meaningfully lower, which makes it an entry point to Eanes ISD at absolute lower dollars.

Toro Canyon matches modern-architecture-oriented buyers, often from the Bay Area tech wealth cohort or from newer custom construction backgrounds. The architectural variety is wider than the more traditional Davenport Ranch, and the lot sizes are often larger.

The right Westlake purchase is the one that matches the pod to the household, not the one that matches a specific square footage to a specific price per square foot.

The Eanes ISD elementary variable

All six Eanes elementaries operate at a strong Texas Education Agency rating level, and all converge at Hill Country Middle or West Ridge Middle in sixth grade, then Westlake High in ninth. For most families the specific elementary matters for three reasons: facility age and quality, parent community culture, and the geographic walkability or drive from the specific home to the elementary.

Barton Creek Elementary primarily serves Barton Creek and south Davenport Ranch. Bridge Point Elementary primarily serves Rob Roy, Rob Roy West, and northwest Westlake. Cedar Creek Elementary primarily serves north Westlake and Toro Canyon. Eanes Elementary primarily serves the original village and central Westlake. Forest Trail Elementary primarily serves Lost Creek and south village. Valley View Elementary primarily serves southwest Westlake and the Lakeway-edge pockets.

Attendance zone lines sometimes cut through subcommunities, which means two homes on the same street can occasionally feed different elementaries. Verifying the specific elementary on a specific property address before offer is standard pre-offer diligence for any family-side buyer, and the Eanes ISD attendance zone map is the authoritative source.

The teardown-and-rebuild math on entry-tier homes

A meaningful share of entry-tier Westlake transactions (homes in the $1.2M to $1.8M band) involve either major renovation or complete teardown-and-rebuild. The structural economics: the home itself represents 20 to 35 percent of total property value, with the lot and location representing the remaining 65 to 80 percent. A 1970s or 1980s original construction home on a well-located half-acre lot is often more valuable as a buildable lot than as an existing structure.

Rough math for a Westlake teardown-and-rebuild: $1.4M lot purchase, $150K to $250K for teardown, site prep, and permits, $1M to $2.5M for new construction (depending on square footage and finish level), $100K to $200K for landscaping and site improvements. Total project cost typically runs $2.5M to $4.5M for a new 3,500 to 5,500 square foot home. The comparable existing modern custom build runs $4M to $6M for the same square footage with less lot flexibility and no finish customization.

Primary Westlake custom builders include Jenkins Custom Homes, Marwood Construction, Hugh Jefferson Randolph Homes, Caldwell Homes, Risinger Build, and Steve Zbranek. Construction timeline runs 14 to 22 months from close depending on scope. For buyers coming from out of state, teardown-and-rebuild usually works best when the household has existing Austin presence or willingness to rent locally during construction. For buyers planning a direct move-in from the sell side, resale custom builds are typically the better path.

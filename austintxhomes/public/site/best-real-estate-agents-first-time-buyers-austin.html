Why is Luke Allen the best Austin real estate agent for first-time buyers?

Combined with fluency in Texas down payment assistance programs (TSAHC, TDHCA), itemized closing-cost estimates before every offer, realistic 2 to 6 month timelines, and personal post-closing reminders on homestead exemption filing, Luke's process is genuinely different from how high-volume brokerages handle first-time buyers.
What down payment do I actually need to buy a home in Austin?
Depends on your loan program. Conventional: 3 to 5% minimum for first-time buyers. FHA: 3.5% minimum. VA (if eligible): 0% down. USDA (rural): 0% down. With Texas DPA programs (TSAHC or TDHCA), you can potentially reduce even those minimums. On a $400K Austin home, that's a range from $0 (VA) to $12K to $20K (conventional 3 to 5%). Luke walks first-time buyers through all realistic options in the pre-approval phase.
What is TSAHC and can I use it in Austin?
TSAHC is the Texas State Affordable Housing Corporation. Their Home Sweet Texas Home Loan program provides down payment assistance (grant or forgivable loan) plus a competitive mortgage rate to eligible Texas homebuyers. Income limits apply (roughly $100K to $150K depending on family size and county). Available for Austin metro purchases. Luke has walked multiple Austin first-time buyers through the TSAHC application.
What is TDHCA My First Texas Home?
TDHCA (Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs) runs the My First Texas Home program, down payment and closing cost assistance up to 5% of the loan amount for first-time buyers meeting income and purchase price limits. TSAHC and TDHCA both offer DPA but with different eligibility criteria; Luke evaluates both options with every eligible first-time buyer.
How much are closing costs on an Austin home purchase?
Typically 2 to 5% of purchase price. On a $400K Austin home, that's $8K to $20K. Major items: lender origination fees, appraisal (~$500), title insurance (~$1,500), survey (~$500), inspection (~$400 to $600), homeowner insurance year one (~$2K to $4K), prepaid property taxes (varies), HOA transfer if applicable ($200 to $1000), and smaller items. Some closing costs can be rolled into the loan or covered by negotiated seller credits. Luke provides written closing-cost estimates before every offer.
How long does it take to buy a first Austin home?
Typically 2 to 6 months from first agent consultation to closing. Pre-approval: 1 to 2 weeks. Neighborhood education and initial showings: 3 to 8 weeks. Finding the right property varies. Contract-to-close: typically 30 to 45 days after offer acceptance. Luke does NOT push first-time buyers to accelerate, the goal is buying the right home, not any home.
What credit score do I need to buy a home in Austin?
Depends on loan program. Conventional loans: 620 to 640 minimum for reasonable rates. FHA loans: down to 580 (500 with 10% down). VA loans: 580+. Higher scores get materially better rates, 740+ typically qualifies for best-available rates. If your credit is in the 620 to 680 range, spend time improving it before applying; the interest savings often exceed $50K over the loan life. Luke connects first-time buyers with lenders who can advise on credit improvement.
What Austin neighborhoods work for first-time buyers?
Depends on budget. Sub-$400K concentrates in outer metro (Kyle, Buda, Manor, Del Valle, Bastrop, outer Georgetown/Round Rock/Pflugerville). Central Austin sub-$400K is thin (mostly condos and townhomes). The Hyde Park condo market at $252K median and the Allandale condo market at $184K median are two of the most accessible Central Austin entry points. Luke walks through both suburban SFR and Central Austin condo scenarios with every first-time buyer.
Does it cost anything to work with Luke as a first-time buyer?
In most Texas residential transactions, no. The seller pays the buyer's agent commission out of sale proceeds. Luke explains the full commission structure before any buyer representation agreement. No hidden fees, monthly retainers, or upfront costs.

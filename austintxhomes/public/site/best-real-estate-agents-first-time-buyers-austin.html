What Matters for First-Time Buyers

The First-Time Buyer
Agent Framework

First-time buyers face a specific structural problem: the real estate industry's commission structure rewards fast transactions, but first-time buyers legitimately need more time and more education than repeat buyers. Many Austin agents optimize for the transaction; the good ones optimize for the client. Here's how to tell the difference.

01

Explanation Over Pressure

You should NEVER feel rushed into an offer, an escalation clause, or a specific loan product. Every first-time buyer decision should be preceded by a clear explanation of the tradeoffs and enough time to think. Any agent who uses "you'll lose the house if you don't decide right now" pressure tactics on a first-time buyer is optimizing for their commission, not your outcome.

02

DPA Program Fluency (TSAHC + TDHCA + City-Specific)

Texas offers robust down payment assistance through TSAHC (Texas State Affordable Housing Corporation) Home Sweet Texas Home Loan, TDHCA (Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs) My First Texas Home program, and city-specific Austin programs. Most large-brokerage agents don't know these programs well because their client base skews to higher-income repeat buyers. First-time-buyer specialists like Luke know them cold.

03

Realistic Closing Cost Breakdown BEFORE You Offer

Closing costs typically run 2–5% of purchase price on top of the down payment — around $10K–$25K on a $500K Austin home. A good agent shows you the itemized estimate BEFORE you write an offer, so you know exactly what cash you'll need at closing. Luke provides written cost estimates as part of every first-time buyer pre-offer consultation.

04

Realistic Timeline (Not "Buy This Weekend")

First-time Austin buyers typically need 2–6 months from first consultation to closing — pre-approval, market education, targeted showings, offer/counter cycles, inspection, appraisal, and closing coordination. Agents pushing for faster timelines are often optimizing for commission velocity. Luke works at YOUR pace.

05

Full Cost Transparency: Lenders, Inspectors, Title, Contractors

A good first-time-buyer agent has a network of trustworthy Austin lenders, inspectors, title companies, and repair contractors — and discloses every referral compensation relationship. Luke's referral network is compensation-neutral: no lender, inspector, title company, or contractor pays Luke to recommend them, and any exceptions would be disclosed upfront.

06

Post-Closing Setup You Won't Think of on Your Own

Texas homestead exemption filing (must be filed by April 30 the year after purchase for full year-one savings), property tax protests, HOA transfers, insurance updates, and homeowner tax deductions all matter after closing. A first-time-buyer specialist reminds you of these; a transactional agent doesn't. Luke does personal check-ins for the first year post-closing.

Before you look at a single property, you need a pre-approval from a lender — not just a "pre-qualification" (which is meaningless). Targeted Showings (Week 3–8)

05

Offer and Contract (Week 4–8)

When you find a property you want, Luke drafts an offer with terms that protect your interests — inspection contingency, financing contingency, appraisal contingency, and appropriate earnest money. Inspection, Appraisal, and Closing (Week 8–12)

Homestead exemption filing (by April 30 the year after purchase), property tax protest (annually, first opportunity January of year after purchase), HOA transfer if applicable, and homeowner insurance review. First-Time Buyer Reality

Buying Your First Home
in Austin Right Now

The 2026 Austin metro market is meaningfully more first-time-buyer-friendly than any market since 2018. Metro-wide active inventory sits at approximately 22,000 for-sale listings — the highest in a decade — and price reductions on active listings are running at roughly 47%. Median list price hovers near $470,000 metro-wide, with 9,000+ active listings priced under $400K. First-time buyers have real selection, real negotiation leverage, and (with the right DPA program) achievable down payment paths.

That said, the Austin market still has structural traps for first-time buyers. Property taxes in Texas are among the highest in the country (~2% of assessed value on average, with wide variance by taxing jurisdiction). Homeowner insurance in Central Texas has become materially more expensive since 2023 due to hail and wind exposure. HOA fees on new-construction Austin homes can add $50–$300+ per month to your carrying cost. First-time buyers who've only budgeted for down payment + monthly PITI are frequently caught off guard by the full cost of Austin homeownership. Luke walks every first-time buyer through the full cost picture in the pre-approval phase.

For Austin first-time buyers specifically evaluating neighborhoods, the sub-$500K segment concentrates most heavily in outer metro areas — Kyle, Buda, Bastrop, Manor, Del Valle, Georgetown's outer zip codes, and portions of Round Rock and Pflugerville. Central Austin sub-$500K inventory is thin (mostly condos and townhomes). The Hyde Park condo market at $252K median and the Allandale condo market at $184K median are two of the most accessible Central Austin entry points for first-time buyers who prioritize inner-loop location.

Frequently Asked Questions

First-Time Austin Buyer
Questions Answered

What down payment do I actually need to buy a home in Austin?
Depends on your loan program. Conventional loans: 3-5% minimum for first-time buyers. FHA loans: 3.5% minimum. VA loans (if eligible): 0% down. USDA loans (rural areas): 0% down. With Texas DPA programs (TSAHC or TDHCA), you can potentially reduce even those minimums further. On a $400K Austin home, that means a range from $0 (VA) to $12K-$20K (conventional 3-5%). Luke walks first-time buyers through all realistic options in the pre-approval phase.
What is TSAHC and can I use it in Austin?
TSAHC is the Texas State Affordable Housing Corporation. Their Home Sweet Texas Home Loan program provides down payment assistance (grant or forgivable loan) plus a competitive mortgage rate to eligible Texas homebuyers. Income limits apply (roughly $100K–$150K depending on family size and county) and you must complete a homebuyer education course. Available for Austin metro purchases. Luke has walked multiple Austin first-time buyers through the TSAHC application process.
What is TDHCA and how does it differ from TSAHC?
TDHCA (Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs) runs the My First Texas Home program — down payment and closing cost assistance up to 5% of the loan amount for first-time buyers meeting income and purchase price limits. TSAHC and TDHCA both offer DPA but with different eligibility criteria and structures; the right choice depends on your specific income, family size, and target purchase price. Luke evaluates both options with every eligible first-time buyer client.
How much are closing costs on an Austin home purchase?
Typically 2-5% of the purchase price. On a $400K Austin home, that's $8K-$20K. Major items: lender origination fees, appraisal (~$500), title insurance (~$1,500), survey (~$500), inspection (~$400-$600), homeowner insurance year one (~$2K-$4K), prepaid property taxes (varies), HOA transfer fees if applicable ($200-$1000), and various smaller items. Some closing costs can be rolled into the loan or covered by seller credits negotiated at offer. Luke provides written closing cost estimates before every first-time buyer offer.
How long does it take to buy a home in Austin as a first-time buyer?
Typically 2-6 months from first agent consultation to closing. Pre-approval takes 1-2 weeks. Neighborhood education and initial showings typically take 3-8 weeks. Finding the right property varies — could be your first offer, could be your fifth. Contract-to-close is typically 30-45 days after offer acceptance. Luke does NOT push first-time buyers to accelerate this timeline — the goal is buying the right home, not any home.
What credit score do I need to buy a home in Austin?
Depends on loan program. Conventional loans typically require 620-640 minimum for reasonable rates. FHA loans can go down to 580 (or even 500 with 10% down). VA loans typically require 580+. Higher scores get materially better interest rates — 740+ typically qualifies for best-available rates. If your credit is in the 620-680 range, spend time improving it before applying — the interest rate savings over the loan life often exceed $50K+. Luke connects first-time buyers with lenders who can advise on credit improvement strategies.
Should I buy a starter condo or hold out for a house?
Depends on your specific situation. Central Austin condos (Hyde Park at $252K median, Allandale at $184K median, Zilker at $590K median) offer accessible entry points into desirable neighborhoods but come with HOA fees ($300-$800+/month is common) and typically slower appreciation than SFR. Suburban SFR at similar or slightly higher price points (Kyle, Buda, Round Rock, Pflugerville) offer more space and typically faster appreciation but longer commutes. Luke walks first-time buyers through both scenarios before recommending either.
What Austin ISD schools should first-time buyers with young kids target?
For families planning to send kids to public school, Austin ISD elementary school assignments materially affect neighborhood pricing. Casis (Tarrytown), Lee (Hyde Park), Bryker Woods (78703), Zilker (Zilker), Barton Hills (Barton Hills), and Brentwood (Brentwood/Crestview) are frequently-requested elementary assignments and carry pricing premiums accordingly. Verify the CURRENT boundary map at austinisd.org before any purchase — boundaries shift periodically and don't always follow street names intuitively. Luke pulls the current AISD map for every first-time buyer with school considerations.
Does it cost me anything to work with Luke as a first-time buyer?
In most Texas residential transactions, no. The seller pays the buyer's agent commission out of sale proceeds. Luke will explain the full commission structure before you sign a buyer representation agreement. No hidden fees, no monthly retainers, no upfront costs. If your specific situation involves an unusual commission arrangement, Luke will disclose it upfront in writing.

Where Tesla employees actually buy in Austin

Four submarkets, in the order I see them show up in my client base:

  • Sendero Hills (78724). New construction, master-planned, 20 to 25 minute commute to Giga. This is the most common landing spot for my Tesla clients. New-build inventory is deep, builders have been running incentives, and the community amenities appeal to buyers relocating from out of state.
  • Manor (78653). Similar story to Sendero Hills but further out. Newer construction, more square footage for the money, 25 to 30 minute commute. If your budget wants a 4 bed / 3 bath under $600K, Manor is where you land.
  • Del Valle (78617). The closest of the four to Gigafactory. Commute time drops to 15 to 20 minutes. Inventory is a mix of newer builds and older homes. Best pick if commute is the number one priority.
  • East Austin (78702). A different profile entirely. Older housing stock, walkable to bars and food, closer to downtown. Tesla employees who want an urban lifestyle over a big backyard end up here. Higher price per square foot but you gain the East Austin proximity to everything.

Most of my Tesla clients prefer new construction. That is why Sendero Hills and Manor come up most often. New construction is easier to walk through virtually before you land in Austin, and the builder incentives (rate buydowns, closing cost credits, upgrade packages) have been material in the current market.

The commute to Gigafactory

Twenty to thirty minutes is the range I see clients report from all four submarkets above. SH-130 will get you around the east side quickly if you are willing to pay the toll, and it usually cuts the trip down.

Shift-change congestion at Giga is real but the volume of workers is spread across enough entry points that it does not become a downtown-Austin-commute nightmare. If you have flexibility on shift start times, the commute drops further.

Twenty to thirty minutes to Gigafactory, from a house you actually want to live in, is the tradeoff Tesla employees consistently choose over living closer in older housing stock or older neighborhoods.

The two mistakes I see Tesla employees make

1. Waiting too long to pick a home

The pattern: a Tesla employee lands in Austin, takes their time exploring, feels no urgency, and then their actual move-in deadline sneaks up. They end up rushing the final decision and settling for something suboptimal because they no longer have the runway to be selective.

The fix: start looking earlier than feels necessary. If you know your Tesla start date, we should be looking 45 to 60 days out, not 20. That gives us the space to walk multiple options and negotiate hard on the right one.

2. Renting first and regretting it a year later

I see this over and over. A new Tesla hire decides to rent for a year to "get a feel" for Austin. Twelve months later they call me, ready to buy, and prices in their target submarket have moved 5 to 12 percent in the meantime. The rent they paid disappears, the price they wanted disappears, and their entry point is worse.

My take: buy first. Austin has such a strong rental market that if life changes at Tesla and you need to relocate, you can rent your place out easily and buy again wherever you land. The downside of buying and renting it out later is much smaller than the downside of watching prices move without you.

Tesla RSUs and getting a mortgage

No issue. Tesla comp structure works fine with standard conventional loans. My Tesla clients have not needed any special financing maneuvering. Some lenders are more comfortable underwriting RSU income than others, so I steer buyers to the ones I know handle tech-comp cleanly, but there is no structural mortgage problem with a Tesla comp package.

My take on the market timing right now

Austin is 1 to 2 months from the bottom of this cycle, in my read. I am seeing buyers who sat on the sidelines all summer starting to move on the deals that are currently listed. Sellers who priced correctly are getting activity. Sellers who over-priced are dropping.

Prices will start picking up again as more buyers re-enter. If you have a Tesla start date in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 and are thinking about renting to "wait out the bottom," my honest answer is that the bottom is now, and the window to buy before other Tesla employees pile in is closing.

Bottom line

Sendero Hills, Manor, Del Valle, and East Austin are the four submarkets where my Tesla clients actually end up. Most prefer new construction. The commute to Giga is 20 to 30 minutes from any of them. Start your search 45 to 60 days before your move-in date so you are not rushed at the end. Buy rather than rent. And do not wait for the "obvious" bottom because by then you have already missed it.

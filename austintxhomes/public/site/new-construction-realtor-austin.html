Why it matters

The on-site agent at a builder works for the builder. Not for you.

Every Austin new construction community has a staffed sales center. The person behind the desk is often warm, well-dressed, and licensed in Texas. That licensure does not change the legal reality: their fiduciary duty runs to the builder that pays them. An Austin new construction realtor working for the buyer runs a different playbook on three specific axes.

1. Representation

Independent duty of care

A Texas Realtor hired by the buyer owes loyalty, confidentiality, and full disclosure to the buyer. The builder sales rep cannot owe those duties and still do their job. A buyer touring alone is self-representing against a professional negotiator whose compensation depends on maximizing the builder's gross.

2. Cost

Builder-paid, buyer pays nothing

Every Austin builder budgets a buyer-agent commission (2.5 to 3 percent of base price is typical) separate from the listing price. If the buyer arrives unrepresented, that commission does not come back to the buyer as a discount. It stays with the builder. Hiring Luke captures the dollars that are already allocated.

3. Document complexity

Builder contract, not TREC

New construction contracts in Austin are written by the builder's legal team, not the Texas Real Estate Commission. Completion-date language, warranty terms, escalation clauses, and preferred-lender riders are all written to favor the builder. Independent review catches what matters before the buyer signs.

How it runs

The Luke Allen new construction process, step by step.

A typical Austin new construction purchase with Luke runs on a defined path. No stage is skipped. Each stage protects the buyer against a specific builder playbook that unrepresented buyers routinely miss.

1

Intake and community shortlist

Ten-minute call covering budget, timeline, must-haves, preferred areas, and builder opinions if any. Luke returns a shortlist of three to eight Austin new construction communities that fit, with current pricing, incentive status, and lot availability.

2

First-visit registration

On the first visit to any community, Luke registers the buyer. This locks representation in writing. Buyers who skip this step and walk in alone forfeit the ability to add representation at that community under most Austin builder policies.

3

Incentive stack negotiation

Austin builders rarely discount the base price. They move on the stack: rate buydown, design studio dollars, closing cost credits, lot premium waivers, free upgrades. Luke knows the current standing offer at each major community and what levers each builder actually pulls.

4

Contract and lender review

Builder contracts contain clauses buyers rarely notice until closing. Preferred lender rate buydowns often have a cliff at month 13 or 25. Completion dates are often non-binding. Luke flags each clause before signing and negotiates amendments where the buyer is exposed.

5

Design studio coordination

Design studio upgrades are the single largest profit center for every Austin production builder. Finish-level upgrades routinely carry 3x retail markup. Luke pre-plans the upgrade sheet so the buyer knows what to pick at the studio and what to defer to post-close.

6

Walk-throughs and closing

Pre-drywall walk (verifies rough-in), pre-closing walk (punch list), and final closing. Luke coordinates each with the builder's superintendent, documents defects, and ensures punch items are resolved before funding rather than after.

Can I still use Luke if I already visited a sales center?

Usually yes, as long as the buyer has not signed the builder's registration card or earnest money contract. Builder registration policies vary. Call before the next visit to a different community so representation is set up correctly.

What is the difference between a new construction realtor and a regular realtor?

The contract, the counterparty, and the negotiation structure. A resale Realtor uses the TREC-promulgated contract against an individual seller. A new construction realtor uses the builder's proprietary contract against a corporate seller, with the incentive stack replacing price cuts as the primary lever.

Is the builder sales rep a licensed realtor?

Often, yes. That licensure does not make them the buyer's agent. The builder sales rep is contractually a fiduciary of the builder. Independent representation on the buyer side is a separate hire.

Both. Representation structure and process are the same at $275,000 and at $4,500,000. The builder playbooks differ between tiers; Luke runs the version appropriate to each.

New construction vs the 3-year resale sweet spot

Every Easton Park buyer asks: Should I buy new from Brookfield or an older resale? Here is the honest answer.

The 3 to 4 year old resale in one of the traditional pods usually wins on total value. Here is what you get with a well-kept resale that you do NOT get from a new build:

  • Backyard finished - $8,000 to $20,000 of work depending on size
  • Fence installed - $4,000 to $8,000
  • Blinds throughout - $2,000 to $5,000
  • Garage door opener + outdoor lighting + final grade + mailbox - $1,500 to $3,000
  • Trees and landscaping at 3 to 4 years of growth - $5,000 to $10,000 of real value
  • No construction traffic in front of your house for the next 12 to 24 months
  • Known-quantity foundation and roof - a home that has been through 3+ Texas summers without issue
  • Established HOA community - you can see the actual event calendar and neighbor dynamic instead of guessing

That is $20,000 to $45,000 of real value the new home does not include, plus the intangible of not living in an active construction zone.

Brookfield still makes sense when: they are running an aggressive rate-buydown promotion that a resale cannot match, a specific floor plan you want is not available on the resale market, or you want the full builder warranty stack and the first-owner tax basis. The right answer depends on what Brookfield is running this month.

The 3-year resale sweet spot usually beats new construction, but "usually" is not "always." Which one wins depends on Brookfield's current incentive stack, and that changes every month.

How I negotiate against Brookfield as your buyer's agent

Here is the trap most Easton Park buyers fall into: they walk into the Brookfield sales trailer, hear the base price, and think that is the number they have to negotiate against. It is not. You do not negotiate the base price with Brookfield. You negotiate the incentive stack.

Brookfield holds sticker but adds incentives to move inventory. Rate buydowns. Closing-cost credits. Design-center allowances. Extended warranties. Included appliances. Lot premium waivers. Every one of those is a real dollar off your effective purchase price. Every one is negotiable if you ask the right way at the right time.

Here is what I do differently when I represent a buyer against Brookfield:

  • Pull the current promo before we tour. Brookfield publishes a monthly promo. I check what is running today and what has been signaled for next month.
  • Stack incentives instead of stacking base-price cuts. Ask for the rate buydown AND the closing cost credit AND the design center allowance. Brookfield will often give more in incentive value than they would in a base-price reduction because their internal accounting treats them differently.
  • Time the offer around Brookfield's quarter close. Sales quotas hit at end-of-quarter. Buyers who write a clean offer in the last 10 days of a quarter get better packages than buyers who sign in week one of a new quarter.
  • Ask about lot premiums that can be waived. "Premium" lots (greenbelt-backing, cul-de-sac, larger square footage) carry a $5,000 to $50,000 premium. Some are negotiable and some are not. I know which is which.
  • Include appliance and finish upgrades in the negotiation. Brookfield will often include the washer/dryer or upgrade the fridge to hold the sticker number.
  • Bring a competing resale to the table. If a comparable resale is priced $20K under net-of-incentive Brookfield, that becomes leverage in your Brookfield negotiation. Brookfield hates losing a buyer to a resale down the street.

My Easton Park buyers routinely close on 6 to 15 percent effective discounts against the release-sheet base price once we back out the full incentive value. That is not from beating Brookfield down on sticker. That is from stacking incentives Brookfield would have given away anyway if the buyer knew to ask.

Which pods to buy in

Not all Easton Park is Easton Park. The pods matter for future resale as much as for daily life. My rankings for a buyer today:

  • Traditional single-family pods within walking distance of The Union. This is my default recommendation for a family buyer. Strongest amenity access, deepest resale support, most-established landscaping. Cottages, Bungalows, and the 50-foot Homestead lots all fit here.
  • 50-foot Homestead and Signature lots throughout the community. Room for a real backyard, better resale flexibility, fits the standard suburban family expectation. If Brookfield is running a promo on a Homestead lot, that is the sweet spot.
  • The Villas and Urban Homes. Lower maintenance, smaller yards, shared components. Right for singles, couples, empty-nesters, or anyone who does not want to mow. Slightly softer resale than the 50-foot single-family, but the trade-off works if your life fits the product.
  • The newer Kellam Road phases. Priced competitively today because builder inventory is heavier there. That is both an opportunity (Brookfield will negotiate more aggressively) and a caution (heavy builder inventory pushes down future resale for the next 24 months). Buy in Kellam only if you are getting a real incentive discount and you plan to hold for 7+ years.

The community you are actually buying into

Easton Park was master-planned on walkable, new-urbanist principles you do not get in most Southeast Austin subdivisions. What you are buying is not just a home. It is:

  • The Union - resort pool, fitness center, event space
  • Skyline Park - downtown Austin skyline views and splash pad
  • 13 miles of trails throughout the community
  • On-site elementary school in Del Valle ISD
  • Food-truck nights and live-music events on the community calendar
  • 15 minutes to Tesla Gigafactory, 12 miles to downtown Austin, 8 miles to ABIA airport, minutes to Circuit of the Americas

The buyer profile in Easton Park is honest. Tesla and supplier employees who want a 15-minute commute and new construction feel. Young families and first-time buyers priced out of South Austin who get 12 miles to downtown, a resort pool, and 13 miles of trails for what a fixer costs in 78745. A steady stream of remote workers and airport-adjacent professionals. That mix is what makes the community work - enough demand to keep resale healthy, not so much builder inventory that resale gets crushed.

The HOA reality before you sign

HOA dues vary by pod. Brookfield lists $75 per month for the traditional single-family sections. The Villas and Urban Homes run different structures because of shared-maintenance components. The community is still developer-controlled with build-out continuing in phases across 2,700 planned acres.

What that means for you as a buyer:

  • Read the community documents before you write an offer. I walk my buyers through the resale certificate line by line.
  • Architectural guidelines control what you can do with the exterior. If you plan to add a pool, an extended patio, or a paint change, verify guidelines allow it before you close.
  • Expect ongoing construction traffic and builder signage in newer sections. That is part of buying in a still-developing master-planned community and it does subside.
  • Del Valle ISD is your school district. The on-site elementary is a real amenity. If your kids will be older than elementary during your ownership, look at the Del Valle middle and high school ratings and campus improvements before committing.

My honest take on timing for a buyer right now

Easton Park is an absorbing market. 59 homes are pending against 83 active listings, and recent closes are hitting 100 percent of ask. That does not mean this is a seller-favored market for buyers to avoid. It means Brookfield keeps releasing lots and prices keep grinding higher, not lower. The buyer who waits six months for a better deal usually finds a market where Brookfield's incentive stack has thinned out and prices have crept up.

The buyer who moves now, with the right agent, can lock in a real discount against Brookfield through the incentive-stack negotiation OR a resale at $10K to $15K under net-of-incentive new. The buyer who waits gives up that leverage.

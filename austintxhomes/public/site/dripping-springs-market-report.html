DSISD School Premium

How DSISD School Quality
Affects Dripping Springs Pricing

Dripping Springs ISD is the single largest demand driver in this market. Homes inside DSISD feeders consistently command 10-20% premiums over comparable Hays County homes outside DSISD boundaries - and within DSISD, elementary feeders can shift demand by another 10-15% on otherwise comparable homes. Walnut Springs and Sycamore Springs feeders have historically carried the strongest pricing, but the differential shifts year-to-year based on capacity, new school openings, and specific campus performance. Luke Allen pulls the feeder pattern for every listing because in DSISD, address-level verification is the difference between a home that holds value and a home that does not. See current Dripping Springs homes for sale by feeder, or explore the living in Dripping Springs guide for school detail.

For families relocating from California or the Northeast, the DSISD pull is often the deciding factor between Dripping Springs and other Austin submarkets. Lake Travis ISD (Lakeway, Bee Cave, parts of West Austin) is the closest direct competitor for school quality, and many families end up choosing between LTISD acreage in Bee Cave / Lakeway and DSISD acreage in Dripping Springs. The math typically comes down to commute (Bee Cave is 10-15 min closer to downtown), price (Dripping Springs is generally less expensive at equivalent lot size), and specific community amenities. Dripping Springs has more active new construction than almost any Austin-area submarket. Builders in Belterra, Caliterra, Headwaters, and Sweetwater set the floor on local resale pricing. When builder incentives are heavy, resale pricing softens. Track builder activity to predict resale moves.

Common Questions

Dripping Springs Market FAQ

What is the median home price in Dripping Springs TX?
Approximately $750K as of 2026, varying dramatically by subdivision. Belterra and Caliterra median $650K-$850K. Headwaters median $900K-$1.2M. Acreage estates in La Ventana, Howard Ranch, and Madrone Ranch routinely exceed $1.5M. Smaller homes near Mercer Street start under $500K. Contact Luke Allen for parcel-specific pricing.
Have Dripping Springs home prices gone up?
Yes - from roughly $325K median in 2015 to $750K in 2026, more than doubling. Pace moderated post-2022 with Austin metro normalization but resale durability remains strong because the DSISD anchor and lifestyle pull are structural.
What is the best subdivision in Dripping Springs for resale?
Belterra has the deepest resale market (4,000+ homes, established HOA). Caliterra has strong resale for newer buyers. Headwaters luxury inventory holds value but has thinner comp depth. Howard Ranch and La Ventana acreage estates resell to a smaller wealthier pool - longer days on market but durable pricing.
How does DSISD affect Dripping Springs prices?
10-20% premium over comparable Hays County properties outside DSISD. Within DSISD, elementary feeders shift demand another 10-15%. Walnut Springs and Sycamore Springs historically carry the strongest pricing. Luke Allen verifies feeder assignment for every address.
Is Dripping Springs a buyer’s or seller’s market?
Varies by tier. Sub-$700K is seller-favored with shorter days on market. $1M-$1.5M is balanced. $1.5M+ acreage and luxury is buyer-favored with more negotiation room. Luke Allen tracks absorption rate weekly by subdivision and price band.
What are property tax rates in Dripping Springs?
1.8-2.2% of assessed value, varying by location. City limits properties pay city tax (~0.18%) on top of Hays County (~0.30%) and DSISD (~1.16%). ETJ properties avoid the city portion. On a $1M home, that’s a $1,500-$2,000 annual difference. Luke Allen pulls actual assessments for every address.

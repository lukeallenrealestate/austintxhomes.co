Where Apollo professionals will actually want to live

Four submarkets fit an Apollo relocation, and the right one depends on family stage, whether you prefer city density or single-family neighborhoods, and whether school district is a driving factor. Ordered from most-city to most-suburban:

  • Downtown Austin high-rise condos (78701). The closest Austin equivalent to an Upper East Side or Tribeca apartment lifestyle. Walk to the future Apollo office. No yard, no maintenance, resort-style amenities. Building options include The Independent, The Austonian, 70 Rainey, Fifth & West, and newer construction along Rainey Street. Price range roughly $1.5M to $8M for the finished product Apollo professionals typically want.
  • Tarrytown, Old Enfield, Clarksville (78703). Central West Austin. Walkable single-family neighborhoods, 10 minutes to downtown, established tree canopy, historic character. Austin ISD schools (Casis Elementary is one of the strongest public elementaries in the state, zoned to O. Henry Middle and Austin High). Price range $2M to $10M+ for the tier Apollo families end up in.
  • Westlake / Eanes ISD (78746). Top-performing public school district in Texas. The choice for Apollo families where school district is the driving factor. 15 minutes to downtown across MoPac. Larger lots than Central Austin, hillier terrain, private-club adjacency. Price range $2.5M to $15M+.
  • Barton Creek (78735 / 78746). Country club community adjacent to Barton Creek Country Club and its 72 holes of golf. Popular with private-equity and hedge-fund types who want membership walkable from home. Price range $1.5M to $10M+.

Downtown high-rise for the single Apollo associate or the younger MD who wants NYC-density lifestyle in Texas. Tarrytown or Westlake for the family that wants a house. Barton Creek for the buyer who wants the private club to be part of the daily rhythm.

The math is stark. New York State plus NYC combined income tax runs up to about 14.8 percent for high earners. Texas has no state income tax. For an Apollo associate at $400K, that is roughly $60K a year. For a VP at $1M, roughly $150K. For an MD at $2M or more, the annual difference clears $300K and only grows from there.

Texas property taxes are higher than New York property taxes on the same home price. Travis County effective rate runs about 1.8 to 2.5 percent of assessed value. On a $3M home, that is $54K to $75K a year. That is real. It is also smaller than the income tax savings for every Apollo comp band above the associate level, and there is a homestead exemption plus a 10 percent annual assessment cap that softens the increase over time.

The net remains dramatically in favor of Texas at every Apollo comp band. That is not an opinion. It is the math.

The school district conversation

For Apollo families with school-age kids, this is often the deciding factor. Three public options and three private options are worth knowing about:

Public.

  • Eanes ISD (Westlake). Top-performing public school district in the Austin metro and one of the top in Texas. If your budget supports Westlake pricing, Eanes ISD is the strongest single decision an Apollo family with school-age kids can make.
  • Austin ISD - Casis Elementary (Tarrytown, 78703). Consistently rated among the strongest public elementaries in AISD. Zoned to O. Henry Middle and Austin High School. Central location, established neighborhoods.
  • Austin ISD - Bryker Woods Elementary (78703). Adjacent to Casis, comparable strength.

Private.

  • St. Andrew's Episcopal School. K through 12, established, competitive admissions.
  • Regents School of Austin. K through 12, classical Christian curriculum, competitive admissions.
  • St. Michael's Catholic Academy. 9 through 12, Catholic, long-established.

All three privates have waitlists. If private is on the table for your Apollo relocation, apply the moment you know the move is happening. Don't wait until you have an address.

Rent first or buy immediately?

My default recommendation for an Apollo relocation is buy. The property tax and mortgage interest deduction math beats paying Austin rent, which is higher than most NYC renters expect for the equivalent unit downtown. Austin has a strong rental market broadly, so if life at Apollo changes or the move does not work out, renting the property is straightforward.

The exception is anyone who genuinely wants six months to get to know Austin before committing to a specific submarket. In that case, rent a downtown high-rise for six months (Rainey Street, 2nd Street District, Warehouse District) and use that time to decide between Downtown, Tarrytown, Westlake, or Barton Creek for the eventual buy. I steer relocation renters to buildings I know are easy to exit at the end of a lease.

A realistic timeline for an Apollo move

  • Weeks 1-2 after you know the move is happening: Initial call. Submarket conversation. School district decision if you have kids. Budget range set.
  • Weeks 3-6: Property tours if you can fly down, otherwise virtual tours. Serious short-list emerges.
  • Weeks 6-8: Offer + negotiation.
  • Weeks 8-14: Under contract, inspection, appraisal, title, closing.
  • Weeks 14-18: Move in, homestead exemption filing, utility setup, kids enrolled if you had a family move.

Total time from "we are moving to Austin" to "keys in hand" is realistically 12 to 18 weeks. Faster if you already know your submarket. Slower if you want to rent first for six months.

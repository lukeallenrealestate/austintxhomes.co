Executive Summary

Six findings that define Austin in Q3 2026

  1. The affordability line is now $500K: 58% of Austin metro for-sale inventory is priced under $500,000 (14,358 of 24,708 listings), meaning the majority of the market is still reachable for households making $130K to $175K.
  2. Median list prices span 8.5× across 35 miles of the metro: from $164K in Elgin (78621) to $1.4M in Westlake / Eanes ISD (78746) , the widest submarket spread of any Texas metro.
  3. Hutto is now the metro's newest housing corridor: average year built across the 100 lowest-priced Hutto listings is 2020. Combined with West Campus / UT's 1975 average, that's a 45-year housing-stock age spread from the newest to oldest submarket.
  4. New construction is 11.6% of for-sale inventory (2,818 listings) , a meaningful supply-side signal for buyers who want lock-and-leave finishes and full builder warranties.
  5. Buy-to-rent inventory ratio is 3:1 (24,360 for-sale vs. 8,068 for-rent), which is materially healthier for buyers than the 5:1 ratio at the 2022 peak. Rental supply is finally catching up.
  6. The biggest submarket by inventory volume is Leander (78641) at 848 listings , reflecting the sustained demand corridor along the northern edge of the metro.

Price Tier Distribution

24,708 for-sale listings, distributed across six commonly-used price bands. The concentration in the $300K,$500K tier (32%) means the median Austin buyer's real competition is other buyers in the same band, not the luxury tier that dominates news coverage.

Section 3

Price vs. Age of Housing Stock

Each dot is a submarket. Age of housing stock (x-axis) is a strong-but-not-perfect predictor of median price (y-axis). Older inner-loop neighborhoods (West Campus, Hyde Park, Crestview) trade in different bands than expected because location, walkability, and school district dominate their pricing.

Section 4

Feature Share

Three uncommon features , new construction, cul-de-sac, and waterfront , collectively make up about 21.2% of Austin metro inventory. Each is small enough that active buyers looking for these features are essentially in a niche market of their own.

Methodology & Data Notes

Source. All data was pulled from live ACTRIS (Austin/Central Texas Regional Information Service) MLS on July 15, 2026 via the same public API that powers austintxhomes.co/search. Every count and per-ZIP total in this report is reproducible by hitting austintxhomes.co/api/properties/search?forRent=false&zip=<ZIP>.

Median calculation. Per-ZIP median list price is computed from the 100 lowest-priced active listings in that ZIP, sorted ascending, taking the middle value. For ZIPs with under 100 total listings, the true median is used. For ZIPs with hundreds of listings, this approach mildly underweights the high end , the actual metro-wide median is likely 3-5% higher than the ZIP-median averages would suggest.

Feature counts. "New construction," "cul-de-sac," and "waterfront" counts are from the same API's boolean filters. Cul-de-sac matches multiple spellings against listing remarks , see the buyer negotiation guide for more on how these signal filter to buyers.

Time coverage. Data snapshot is Q3 2026 (as of the July 15, 2026 pull date). We plan to publish an updated report each quarter with year-over-year change figures once we have four quarters of matched data.

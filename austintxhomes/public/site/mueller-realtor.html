The origin story that explains everything else

From municipal airport to 711-acre master-planned community

Mueller sits on the site of the former Robert Mueller Municipal Airport, which operated 1930 to 1999. The redevelopment master plan brought a specific set of design priorities that still shape every daily-life decision inside the community.

1999
Airport closes
Robert Mueller Municipal Airport operations end as Austin-Bergstrom opens, leaving a 711-acre central Austin site.
2004
Master plan approved
Catellus Development selected as master developer. Mixed-use, walkable, 20 percent affordable housing commitment built in from the start.
2007
Dell Children's opens
Dell Children's Medical Center of Central Texas opens as the first LEED Platinum hospital in the world. Anchor institution.
LEED
Platinum certified
First US master-planned community at this scale to achieve LEED Neighborhood Development Platinum certification.

The civic infrastructure that defines daily life

What living at Mueller actually looks like

Six anchor features turn Mueller into a walkable urban community rather than a drive-everywhere suburb. Most residents use the community as their primary retail-dining-recreation base.

Park

Mueller Lake and Lake Park

30 acres of central green space with Mueller Lake at the heart. Playgrounds, trails, water features, event lawns, outdoor movies, community programming. The community's living room.

Town center

Mueller Central

Walkable retail and dining district with Alamo Drafthouse Cinema, HEB grocery, approximately 20 restaurants and casual dining options, Sunday farmers market, and resident-serving services (dry cleaner, hair, dental, veterinary).

Healthcare

Dell Children's + Dell Medical School

Dell Children's Medical Center opened 2007 as the first LEED Platinum hospital in the world. Dell Medical School at UT Austin and UT Health Austin outpatient care sit adjacent. Best-in-class pediatric and specialty medical infrastructure within walking distance.

Culture

Thinkery children's museum

On-site children's museum operating inside the community. Combined with the Alamo Drafthouse and the community events calendar, Mueller offers meaningful cultural infrastructure without needing to drive downtown.

Mobility

Walkable + bikeable + transit

Hike-and-bike trails connect the full 711-acre footprint. CapMetro bus routes serve the community. Mueller is one of few Austin addresses where meaningfully car-light or car-free living is practical for day-to-day life.

Downtown

10-15 minutes to downtown Austin

3 to 4 miles northeast of downtown via I-35 or Airport Boulevard. 10 to 15 minutes drive to downtown offices (Google, Oracle Lakeshore, startup ecosystem), 10 to 15 minutes to UT campus, 15 to 25 minutes to Tesla Gigafactory southeast.

Who Mueller typically pulls

Mueller pulls inbound from four distinct demographic streams, each organized around a specific daily-life priority.

Dell Medical School and healthcare professionals. Physicians, residents, Dell Medical School faculty, Dell Children's Medical Center staff, UT Health Austin researchers and administrators. Walking distance to work at a top-tier teaching hospital infrastructure is a specific, hard-to-replicate value proposition. Mueller pulls meaningful medical-professional demographics for this reason.

Downtown Austin tech professionals wanting central location with master-planned structure. Google downtown, Oracle Lakeshore, Meta Austin, Indeed, and the broader downtown startup ecosystem employees who want master-planned-community predictability (consistent housing stock quality, HOA infrastructure, retail walkability) without suburban commute distance. Mueller delivers that bundle better than any other Austin address.

Downsizing from suburban Austin to urban walkability. Empty-nest or near-empty-nest households selling a Westlake, Steiner Ranch, or Lakeway family home and converting to Mueller condo or townhome for the walkability, cultural density, and lifestyle shift. The master-planned structure makes the transition less jarring than moving to a conventional condo building downtown.

Younger professionals and DINKs choosing urbanism over suburb. First-time or second-time buyers in their late 20s through early 40s who specifically do not want a suburban master-planned community (Steiner Ranch, Belterra, Rough Hollow) but do want master-planned community infrastructure (consistent amenity, HOA-managed shared spaces, community programming). Mueller is the only Austin address delivering that combination at scale.

How Mueller compares to other Austin options

Vs. Westlake. Mueller is central Austin AISD, not Eanes ISD. Mueller has walkable urbanism and condo-through-single-family mix; Westlake is detached single-family only with no walkable retail center. Mueller is $100K-$500K lower at comparable square footage. Westlake wins on school district (Eanes consistently top-ranked), lot sizes, and country-club density. Mueller wins on walkability, commute to downtown, property type optionality, and healthcare proximity.

Vs. Steiner Ranch. Mueller is 711 acres central Austin; Steiner Ranch is 4,600 acres NW Austin suburban. Mueller delivers urban-ish walkability; Steiner Ranch delivers master-planned suburban amenity density including UT Golf Club, equestrian center, Lake Austin frontage. Mueller is downtown-commute-first; Steiner Ranch is school-feeder-first. Different households, different answers.

Vs. downtown condo buildings. Mueller delivers master-planned community structure (consistent amenity, HOA governance, event programming, retail walkability) that individual downtown condo buildings do not. Mueller is further from downtown than a Rainey Street or 2nd Street District condo but inside the community you walk to the farmers market, cinema, HEB, restaurants, and park. For buyers who want urban walkability without the downtown condo building constraints (shared elevator, HOA fees without community amenity, limited outdoor space), Mueller is the alternative.

Pick Mueller when the right answer to "do you want suburban or urban" is "both at the same time, inside one master-planned community, close to downtown."

Pemberton Heights was one of Austin's first planned suburban developments (1928) and it still shows. The curving streets, deep front-yard setbacks, and mature oak canopy give the neighborhood a visual coherence that most Austin neighborhoods don't have. That coherence has a real market effect: Pemberton Heights homes appreciate more predictably than neighborhoods with heterogeneous stock, because buyers know exactly what "Pemberton character" looks like and are willing to pay for it consistently.

Luke's Take on Pemberton Heights

Pemberton's consistent architectural stock means comps here are more reliable than in most 78703 neighborhoods. What is the median home price in Pemberton Heights?

Standard 3-4 bedroom homes in Pemberton Heights trade $1.4M to $2.5M. Larger renovated or estate-scale homes reach $3M-$6M. Homes inside the Casis Elementary catchment carry a 5-10% premium over otherwise-similar homes just outside the boundary.

Is Pemberton Heights in the Casis Elementary catchment?

Most of Pemberton Heights falls in Casis Elementary's catchment, but not all. The catchment boundary runs through parts of the neighborhood in ways that surprise buyers. Always verify the exact address with Austin ISD before assuming Casis assignment — the difference can be $75K-$150K in value.

Why is Pemberton Heights' architecture so consistent?

It was one of Austin's first planned suburban developments, laid out in 1928 with deed restrictions on setbacks, curving street patterns, and lot proportions. That consistency has persisted for nearly a century and is a big part of why the neighborhood's home values appreciate so predictably.

What are common condition issues in Pemberton Heights homes?

Most Pemberton stock dates to the 1920s-1950s, so common items are: kitchen and bathroom updates (buyers above $1.8M expect these done), foundation shifts on pier-and-beam construction, electrical panel upgrades (older knob-and-tube in some houses), and original single-pane windows (aesthetically valued but energy-inefficient).

How does Pemberton Heights compare to Tarrytown or Old Enfield?

Pemberton is more architecturally uniform (planned garden suburb, one design era). Tarrytown is broader and more varied stock. Old Enfield is older (1910s core), more estate-scale, and more heterogeneous. All three sit in similar $1.4M-$3M price bands for standard homes; the character difference is what drives buyer preference.

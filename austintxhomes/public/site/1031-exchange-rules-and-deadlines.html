1031 Exchange Rules and Deadlines: A Day-By-Day Playbook

Day minus 30, day zero, day 45, day 180. Everything in a 1031 exchange works backward from three dates and forward from one. The calendar below maps the whole timeline, from before-the-sale setup through the Form 8824 filing that closes the chapter.

Day -30Engage the QI
Day 0Sold Property Closes
Day 45Identify in Writing
Day 180Close Replacement

Day minus 30

Setup before the sale.

The exchange begins before the relinquished sale closes. The QI must be engaged, the exchange agreement signed, and the cooperation clause inserted into the sale contract. If the relinquished sale closes without a QI in place, the exchange cannot be reconstructed after the fact. Proceeds that touch the seller's account, even for a minute, trigger constructive receipt and permanently disqualify the transaction.

Investors planning a 1031 should also have their CPA calculate the deferred gain and depreciation recapture figures before the sale so the replacement value target is known. Buying under the sold value creates taxable boot. For strategic context, see the Austin 1031 exchange guide. For QI selection see Qualified Intermediary scorecard.

Day zero

Relinquished property closes.

Closing day on the sold property starts both clocks. The 45-day identification window and the 180-day close window both run from the exact date title transfers on the relinquished property, not from the date of contract or the date of offer. Proceeds route from the title company directly to the QI's segregated account, not to the exchanger.

Day 1 through 14

Build the identification shortlist.

The first two weeks after day zero are the highest-leverage time in the whole exchange. Replacement candidates discovered later typically do not clear underwriting in time. Luke Allen runs this window by pulling every Austin property matching the investor's target asset class, price band, and submarket, then narrowing to a tour-ready list of six to ten within four business days of engagement.

Day 15 through 44

Visit, underwrite, negotiate.

Days 15 through 30 are typically property tours, often compressed into two or three Austin visits. Days 30 through 44 are offers and counteroffers on the three properties that will be formally identified. The identification does not require a signed contract on any property; the written notice only requires that the properties be sufficiently described (address and parcel number, or address and APN).

Day 45

Identification deadline.

Written identification must be in the QI's possession by midnight of day 45. The IRS counts calendar days, not business days. Weekends and federal holidays count. A day 45 that falls on a Sunday is still day 45. The identification notice must be signed by the exchanger and delivered in a form the QI accepts. Most QIs accept email with a scanned signature; some require original signature by overnight courier. Confirm the QI's required delivery method on day 1.

The identification can use one of three rules. The 3-Property Rule allows up to three properties regardless of combined value. The 200% Rule allows any number of properties as long as combined fair market value does not exceed 200% of the relinquished sale price. The 95% Rule allows unlimited identification but requires the exchanger to close on at least 95% of the aggregate identified value.

Day 46 through 170

Contract, inspection, lender, title.

Once identification is filed, the exchange converts into a conventional purchase transaction on one of the identified properties, with the wrinkle that the QI must be assigned into the contract and hold the funds through close. Lender timelines become the binding constraint for most investors. A residential investor loan typically needs 30 to 45 days from fully executed contract to close. A commercial loan can need 60 to 75. Working backward from day 180 sets the latest acceptable contract execution date.

Day 180

Close deadline.

The replacement property must close on or before midnight of day 180, again on calendar days. There is one narrow extension available: if the exchanger's tax return due date (including extensions) falls before day 180, the earlier date becomes the hard deadline. In practice this almost never binds for calendar-year taxpayers because the standard April 15 due date plus the six-month extension to October 15 gives most exchanges a longer window than 180 days.

Post-close

Form 8824 and depreciation carryover.

The exchange is reported on IRS Form 8824, filed with the exchanger's return for the tax year the relinquished property closed. Deferred gain reduces the basis of the replacement property by the amount deferred. Depreciation on the replacement property runs on two schedules: the carried-over basis continues on its original schedule, and any additional basis from new cash into the deal starts a fresh schedule. Both tracks live together on the depreciation worksheet.

Pin the calendar on the wall.

Pin the calendar on the wall. Day zero, day 45, day 180. Everything else works backward from those three dates. Investors who treat the exchange as a project with a critical path meet their deadlines routinely. Investors who treat it as a real estate transaction with a tax wrapper usually run into trouble on day 42. If an exchange is in your near future, start the calendar before the sold property closes.

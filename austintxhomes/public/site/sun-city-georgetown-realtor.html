The 65-plus Texas property tax freeze is the quiet case for Sun City

Texas law locks the school district portion of property tax at the dollar level reached when a homeowner turns 65 (or buys a Texas home after 65). The freeze does not expire. The home can appreciate significantly over the next 20 years and the school tax amount stays locked.

Worked example. A 67-year-old purchases a $500,000 Sun City home in 2026. First-year property tax: $11,000 total, roughly $7,500 school district and $3,500 city, county, MUD. The $7,500 school district portion locks at $7,500 immediately. If the home appreciates to $800,000 by 2036, the market would normally produce $12,000 in school tax at the new value. Instead, the school tax stays at $7,500. Over 10 years the compounded savings are typically $25,000 to $50,000 relative to a non-frozen homeowner.
Who qualifies
Any Texas homeowner with at least one person age 65-plus living in the home. Surviving spouse keeps the freeze if the 65-plus homeowner dies, provided the spouse is 55 or older.
What stays frozen
The school district portion of property tax, which is typically the largest single line item (60-70 percent of total property tax). City, county, MUD, and other jurisdictions still fluctuate but are capped by the homestead 10 percent rule.
How to activate
File the Over-65 Homestead Exemption with Williamson County Appraisal District in the first year of eligibility. The exemption also activates the freeze automatically. No annual renewal required once filed.
Portability
If you move to another Texas home after 65, the frozen tax amount transfers at the proportional ratio of the new home's school tax to what would be the unfrozen amount, so the next home gets the same percentage reduction.

The three primary sections inside Sun City Georgetown

Sun City Georgetown is not a monolithic development. It grew in phases across three primary sections that have distinct ages, character, and amenity associations.

White Wing (original section, 1995-2005 primary construction). The oldest and most established pod, built around the White Wing Golf Club. Older housing stock (1990s and early 2000s construction), mature landscaping, lower price per square foot, and the strongest social density because residents have been there longest. White Wing attracts buyers who prioritize established community over new-build finishes. Pricing typically runs 10 to 20 percent below Legacy Hills for comparable square footage.

Legacy Hills (2005-2020 construction). The middle-generation pod, built around the Legacy Hills Golf Club. Housing stock from mid-2000s through late 2010s with more current layouts than White Wing. The 2010s section specifically delivers larger great rooms, more open floor plans, and larger primary suites than the original 1990s White Wing floor plans. Legacy Hills is where most mid-tier buyers end up.

Cowan Creek (2015-ongoing construction). The newest section with Del Webb still actively building through 2026 and beyond. Current-generation layouts, builder-grade finishes with upgrade options, builder incentive packages that fluctuate quarterly, and the newest amenity center (Cowan Creek Amenity Center opened 2015 with a dedicated gym, pool, and clubhouse). Cowan Creek typically carries a new-construction premium of 10 to 15 percent over Legacy Hills resale on comparable square footage.

The typical buyer coming from out of state

Sun City Georgetown's inbound migration map parallels the state funnel pages elsewhere on this site. The strongest inbound streams come from California (Bay Area, LA County, Orange County), Illinois (Chicago metro and collar counties), New York (Long Island, Westchester, Upstate), New Jersey (North Jersey suburbs), Massachusetts (MA metro and Berkshires), Washington (Seattle area), Colorado (Front Range), Oregon (Portland), and Minnesota (Twin Cities). The common thread is a 60-to-75-year-old household selling a long-held home (often $800K-$2M in appreciated equity) and purchasing a right-sized Sun City home in cash or with meaningful down payment.

The typical motivations rank as follows based on client conversations: climate (specifically avoiding Northeast or Midwest winters), tax exposure (specifically escaping state income tax on Social Security, pension, and IRA distributions), community engagement (ready-made social infrastructure that solo retirement living in a conventional neighborhood does not provide), proximity to adult children (Austin tech employment pulls grandchildren within driving distance), and healthcare (the Austin-St. David's-Scott and White medical corridor is among the better retirement-destination healthcare systems in the US).

What this realtor relationship actually looks like

Sun City Georgetown buyer representation is different from standard Austin buyer representation in three ways. First, familiarity with the floor plans matters. Del Webb builds and has built dozens of named floor plans across the three sections (Steel Creek, Hearthside, Dartmouth, Rushmore, Oakdale, Mystique, and many others). Knowing which floor plan a resale listing represents and understanding its typical upgrade package, resale premium, and typical issues (slab settlement, roof age, HVAC generation) materially affects pricing analysis and inspection focus.

Second, the 65-plus tax freeze mechanics require specific attention. The freeze locks at purchase for buyers already over 65, but buyers in their late 50s or early 60s need to understand that the freeze does not apply at purchase; it applies when the eligible homeowner turns 65. This is a known structural quirk worth discussing before offer rather than after close.

Third, new construction and resale decisions are tightly linked because Del Webb is still actively building. Current builder incentive packages (closing cost credits, rate buydowns, interior design allowances) fluctuate quarterly and are negotiable in ways most buyers do not realize. For a buyer debating new construction versus resale, understanding the current builder incentive stack affects whether the 10-15 percent new-construction premium pencils or not.

The best Sun City Georgetown transactions come from buyers who understand which section matches their priorities, what the floor plan deltas actually mean, and how the 65-plus freeze mechanics work before writing the first offer.

Austin and Georgetown proximity in practice

Sun City Georgetown sits on the north side of Georgetown, Texas, about 25 miles from downtown Austin via IH-35. Driving times to key destinations: historic Georgetown Square 10 to 15 minutes, Round Rock Dell Diamond and Round Rock Premium Outlets 15 to 20 minutes, the Domain shopping center in north Austin 25 to 30 minutes, downtown Austin 35 to 45 minutes depending on IH-35 traffic, and Austin-Bergstrom International Airport 45 to 60 minutes.

For a retiree household, the practical pattern tends to be: daily life inside the community (walking, golf, pickleball, clubs), weekly trips to Georgetown Square and Wolf Ranch Town Center for groceries, shopping, and casual dining, monthly or every-few-weeks trips to Austin for cultural events (Bass Concert Hall, ACL Live at Moody Theater, the Blanton Museum, specialty restaurants), specialty medical appointments at Dell Seton or St. David's Medical Center, and travel through ABIA.

Healthcare specifically: St. David's Georgetown Hospital provides general hospital care 10 minutes from Sun City. Scott and White Round Rock Medical Center is 20 minutes south. Dell Seton Medical Center (the primary teaching hospital for Dell Medical School at UT Austin) is 35 to 45 minutes south in central Austin. The Austin medical corridor including MD Anderson, Dell Children's, and the specialty centers along the Barbara Jordan Boulevard spine is also 35 to 45 minutes. For a 60-plus household, this healthcare proximity is a meaningful quality-of-life factor compared to Sun City West (Arizona), Sun City Hilton Head (South Carolina), or The Villages (Florida), all of which trade off healthcare proximity for various other features.

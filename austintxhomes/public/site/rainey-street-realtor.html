Rainey Street is the most consolidated real estate submarket in Austin — a handful of high-rise buildings, all condo product, each with distinct pricing dynamics driven by year built, amenity stack, and view corridor. A Rainey purchase isn't really a "Rainey purchase" — it's a purchase in one of about 10 specific buildings, each with their own HOA, resale patterns, and buyer pool.

The biggest Rainey pricing mistake buyers make is comparing across buildings by price-per-square-foot without controlling for view. A north-facing city-view unit at 70 Rainey trades at a materially different number than a south-facing hill-country-view unit in the same building at the same square footage. If you're not underwriting the specific view, you're not really underwriting Rainey.

Luke's Take on Rainey Street

Rainey Street's specific challenge for sellers right now: the neighborhood is officially in a buyer's condo market. Rate math is hard for buyers financing at 6.5%+; HOA fees average $700-$1,400/month across the district; and the resale supply overhang from 2021-2022 buyers taking losses hasn't fully cleared. Well-priced units still move, but "well-priced" is 10-15% below 2022 asks.

On the buyer side, Rainey is one of Austin's clearest current opportunities. Effective all-in discounts of 12-18% below original ask are achievable on properly-negotiated deals. If you're considering downtown living for 5+ years, the current buy math works better than at any point since 2019.

What is the median condo price on Rainey Street?

Entry 1-bedroom units run $500K to $1.2M. 2-3 bedroom units trade $1.5M to $3M. Penthouse product goes $4M+. Prices vary significantly by building, floor, and view corridor within a single building.

What are HOA fees like on Rainey Street?

Typical range is $700 to $1,400 per month depending on building age, amenity stack, and unit size. Any Rainey condo purchase should include a full HOA financial review — reserve fund status, upcoming special assessments, and historical fee escalation trend all matter for your true carrying cost.

Is Rainey Street a good buy right now?

For 5+ year holds, yes — this is the strongest downtown Austin condo buy math in five years. Effective discounts of 12-18% below original ask are achievable on properly-negotiated deals. For under-2-year holds, transaction costs plus HOA plus continuing repricing risk usually make renting the safer play.

Which Rainey Street building is best?

Depends on your priorities. 70 Rainey is newer with strong amenity stack. The Shore has direct Lady Bird Lake proximity and mature landscaping. Milago is older but has the district's best value pricing. 44 East Ave is technically East Ave but functionally Rainey-adjacent, newer, and view-corridor-friendly. Building selection should be driven by your specific criteria, not "which is best."

Should I finance a Rainey Street condo?

Confirm your lender approves condo loans for your target building. Some Rainey buildings have limited financing options due to owner-occupancy ratios, HOA financial health, or ongoing litigation. This should be verified building-specific before you commit to a purchase timeline.
